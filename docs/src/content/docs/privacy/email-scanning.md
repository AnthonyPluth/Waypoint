:::note[What’s built]
Members connect a Gmail read-only (see [Google OAuth client for Gmail](/Waypoint/start/gmail/)), and Waypoint scans it for bookings in schema.org markup, falling back to a parser for senders without it (Southwest and Disney Cruise Line so far), and queuing what it can’t read in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/). The optional AI suggestions below are built.
:::

Waypoint’s main way of learning about a booking is to read the confirmation email in a Gmail account you connect. The rules it keeps:

- **Read-only.** Waypoint asks Google for read-only access to your mailbox. It can’t send, delete, label or change anything. You can revoke the access at any time in your Google account.
- **Searched on the server.** Waypoint asks Gmail for the ids of all your mail, in any folder or tab (Promotions too) except spam and trash. For each message it then asks Google for only the subject, the sender and a short snippet, and decides from those whether the message is worth downloading, so one-time codes, newsletters, auto-replies, replies and shopping mail (orders, shipping, returns, receipts) are never downloaded and nothing of a skipped message is kept. A travel word in the subject, snippet or sender’s name decides, not Gmail’s own categories on their own (a message Gmail files under Purchases or Reservations is read only when it also has a general booking word such as “confirmation” and nothing retail about it, and then goes to Review if it can’t be made into a booking, kept only while that item waits); a message that is read but can’t be made into a booking and has only one travel word, from a sender Waypoint doesn’t know, is dropped without a Review item or a stored copy. A scan works for up to 20 minutes, newest first, and a big mailbox carries on in a follow-up scan that starts straight away until every message has been looked at. You can also pick some of your own Gmail labels (Settings → Mail and AI → Gmail → Choose labels, none by default): every message in a picked label is read for bookings whatever its sender or subject, and a label you delete in Gmail drops out with a note. Gmail’s own labels are never offered, only you can see or change your picks, and no AI assistant can reach them. A forwarded message is read as the original: its sender, subject and body are the original’s, and the forwarder is noted only as the person who passed it on. The original’s sender is whatever the forward’s own header says, so anyone who can email you could write a forward claiming to be a sender you trust; that only ever files a booking into your own mailbox’s trips. Settings shows each scan’s counts (found, read, bookings, couldn’t be read, follow-ups attached, emails attached to an existing booking, skipped, and how many of those were skipped as not travel), and only counts: no subject, sender or text, which is also all that is logged.
- **Bodies are read in memory, and a message is kept only while it’s needed.** A message’s body is fetched and parsed in memory, and Waypoint keeps what it found (a flight number, a time, a confirmation code) and a reference to the message (its Gmail id; for a message from a sender you ignored, that record also keeps the sender’s domain, and stopping the ignore deletes it). It also keeps the message itself, but only while something needs it: a [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/) item waiting for it, or a booking made from it. What is kept is its subject, who it was from and the day, its text and, for an HTML message, its layout and its images: markup rebuilt on the server from a list of safe tags with a safe subset of inline formatting (colours, fonts, sizes, spacing, alignment, tables), and nothing that runs or loads (no scripts, event handlers, style blocks, forms or remote pictures), plus the images it shows (see [What the sender can learn](#what-the-sender-can-learn)). A long message is kept in full up to a hard limit of 300,000 characters of text and 900,000 characters of markup, and Waypoint says “This email was cut short” only past it. It is stored as one encrypted value, under the same key as the Gmail connection, so a copy of the database or a backup doesn’t show it, images included, without that key. It is deleted, images with it, when its item is dismissed (unless a booking was made from it, which keeps it), when the last booking made from it is removed, when its mailbox is disconnected, and when a member taps **Done** on a trip that ended at least 7 days ago, for that trip’s bookings (see [Done with a trip](/Waypoint/start/trips/#done-with-a-trip)). Mail that made no booking and wasn’t queued for review, and mail from a sender you stopped reviewing, is never kept. The message is never logged, reported or sent to any service other than Gmail’s own API (and the optional AI below, if you turn it on), no AI assistant connected over MCP can read its text and kept markup (never images) unless you switched on “Read email text” for that one connection in Settings (off by default; see [AI assistants](/Waypoint/start/mcp/#reading-email-text-optional)), and the project’s checks hold the code to that: only one module looks inside a message, decryption stays in the storage layer, and the tests scan made-up emails while watching the database (for the text in the clear), the log and every request. The one other place the message can be is your own device: if you set up [offline access](/Waypoint/start/offline/), the messages kept for the bookings on your current trip (their text and formatting, not their images) are part of the copy Waypoint saves there, encrypted, for you only, and cleared when you sign out, when you turn it off and three days after the trip ends.
- **An email for a booking you already have is kept with that booking.** When a message does make a booking and it is the same booking as one the mailbox’s owner can already see (the same kind and confirmation code around the same dates, the same flight number and airports for a flight, not a cancelled one, nothing contradicting it, and nothing new about when or where), Waypoint makes no second booking. The message is kept, encrypted and capped exactly as any other, and shown on that booking beside the others, newest first, and it is kept for as long as that booking exists. Only what the booking lacked is filled in, and a traveller the message names who isn’t on the booking yet is added; nothing you edited changes. A booking the owner can’t see is never matched. The scan’s figures count these as a number (“emails attached to an existing booking”) and nothing else.
- **Follow-up mail is recognised, not kept.** A later email about a booking Waypoint already has (a receipt, a check-in notice, a reminder, the hotel’s reply) is matched to it by the confirmation code in its text, against the bookings the mailbox’s owner can see: it is attached only when the visible bookings with that code from the same company are one reservation (one booking, or the legs of one booking in one trip, with no other code in the text fitting another), never from dates or names alone, and an email whose subject or text says a booking has been cancelled, changed or rescheduled is left for a person. It is recorded as seen with the booking’s id, and nothing else of it is kept: no text, subject or code. The one exception is a follow-up about a parking booking that holds a picture that looks like the gate’s QR code (a small, near-square PNG or JPEG that is not repeated in the message): that picture alone is kept with the booking, encrypted like a kept message’s images, and shown only to those who can see the booking (see [The gate code of a parking booking](/Waypoint/start/trips/#the-gate-code-of-a-parking-booking)). A code that fits only a booking the owner can’t see counts as no match. Items that do wait in Review and name the same code are shown as one row; for that, each such item keeps a keyed hash of its code (HMAC with a key derived from the same key as the stored mail), never the code, and the hash is deleted with the item.
- **Your server only.** The mailbox is read by your own Waypoint, with an OAuth client you create (see [Google OAuth client for Gmail](/Waypoint/start/gmail/)). Nothing goes through a service run by the project.
- **You see only your trips.** A booking is visible to the people on the trip, the person who booked it, and whoever got its confirmation in their own mailbox (the same email in two household members’ mailboxes is one booking, shown to both). Mail Waypoint couldn’t read is the mailbox owner’s alone unless they choose to [share that mailbox’s items with the household](/Waypoint/start/review/#sharing-a-mailboxs-items-with-the-household): then the household sees each one’s subject, who it is from and its day, and can read the message beside the Add by hand form. Anyone who can see a booking can read the email it was made from, and see its images; no one else can.

## What the sender can learn

Opening an email in Waypoint never contacts the sender: the email is shown inline in the page, inside an isolated container that can run no script and may load only images Waypoint itself gives it (the page’s own security policy allows no script from an email and no remote image), so a tracking pixel in a message can’t tell anyone that you looked.

The one thing that changes is when the message is kept (a booking was made from it, or it went to Review). Then your Waypoint server downloads the images the message names, once, so they are there when you open it and still there if the sender removes them. To the sender that is a request from your server’s address for each image address in the message, which may carry whatever the sender put in that address, such as a code that identifies the message. The request has no cookies and nothing about you or Waypoint beyond a generic browser name, and it happens once, not each time someone opens the email. Images attached to the message itself need no download.

What the download will and won’t do:

- Only `https` addresses that resolve to public internet addresses: private, loopback and link-local addresses are refused, including after a redirect or a changed DNS answer, and no proxy is used.
- At most 1 MB for an image, 6 MB and 40 images for a message, and a few seconds for an image and 30 for a message. An image that fails or is refused is left out, and the message still opens.
- Only PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, decided by the file’s contents. SVG is refused, since it can carry script.
- The images are kept encrypted with the message and deleted with it. They are shown only to those who can see the booking or review item, through Waypoint’s own sign-in-protected addresses, and are never reachable by an AI assistant over MCP or sent to the optional AI below. A parking booking also keeps a copy of the one picture chosen as its gate code, sealed the same way, which goes with the booking and with the mailbox, is never decoded, and is never in the offline copy.

Messages kept before this was added show as before (their text and simple formatting), with a line saying the original can’t be shown; Waypoint doesn’t fetch them again on its own.

## How a booking is read

Waypoint tries the cheapest, most reliable method first and falls through (the generic readers run in this order, and the first that yields a complete booking wins):

1. **schema.org markup** (built). Many airlines, hotels, rental companies and railways embed structured data (`FlightReservation`, `LodgingReservation`, `RentalCarReservation`, `TrainReservation`) in the email for Gmail’s own cards. Waypoint reads it directly.
2. **Per-vendor parsers** (started: Southwest Airlines and Disney Cruise Line). For senders without markup, a parser written for that vendor’s email reads its text for the same fields, and for its change and cancellation emails. A parser only ever sees the message’s text in memory, and keeps nothing of it. Disney Cruise Line’s parser reads the booking confirmation: the sailing, its ship, stateroom and deck, each guest’s name (never their Castaway Club number) and the ports of call with their times at each port’s own zone; a re-sent confirmation updates the same booking, a port it doesn’t know goes to review, and its change and cancellation emails aren’t read yet. More vendors come one at a time.
3. **A calendar attachment** (built). A message with a calendar attachment (`.ics`) is read through it before its body is tried by the generic readers. Waypoint reads each event’s summary, start and end (with the event’s own time zone), location, description and unique id with Python’s standard library, in memory, and makes a booking only when the summary or description names a kind: a flight number with two airports it knows, a hotel or stay, a car rental, a train or a cruise. A flight, car, train or cruise event needs a start and end time in a named time zone and a place or route; a stay may be all-day (check-in and check-out times are then assumed). Times are the event’s own and never converted, an event without a kind (a lunch, say) is ignored, and a malformed attachment is skipped without failing the message. The attachment arrives with the message Google returns once the message has passed the first look at its subject, sender and snippet, and is never stored. Like the generic readers, the first booking read this way from a sender you haven’t confirmed goes to Review as a suggestion; PDF attachments are not read.
4. **A generic lodging reader** (built). For a stay whose email has neither markup nor a vendor parser, Waypoint reads labelled lines (Check-in, Check-out, Hotel or Property, Address, Reservation or Confirmation number, in English and Japanese, as lines or table cells), or a start date plus a number of nights. It needs the place and both dates, the check-out after the check-in and no more than 60 nights; dates in cancellation text are ignored, and the times are the property’s own, in the zone worked out from its address. The first booking read this way from a sender you haven’t confirmed goes to Review as a suggestion with the fields it found outlined; adding it trusts that sender, and later mail from there is added without asking.
5. **A generic flight reader** (built). For a flight whose email has neither markup nor a vendor parser, Waypoint looks for a flight number (an airline code that exists in its airline table, then digits), two airports written as three capital letters that exist in its airport table, and the departure and arrival date and time written near them: as labelled lines, as rows of a table, or side by side in two columns. An email with several flights, such as a round trip, gives one booking per flight, each read from its own block with its own airports, date, times and flight number; a flight it can’t complete (no time, the same airport it leaves from, or longer than 30 hours in the air) is counted and sent to Review while the others are kept. It needs both airports, both dates with a year and both times, and an arrival after the departure (compared at each airport’s own zone). The times are the airports’ own, written as the email gives them and never converted; each airport’s zone comes from its table. Dates in cancellation, refund or “booked on” text are ignored, and a confirmation or booking reference is read from its label. The same safeguards as the lodging reader apply: when it can’t read a message it says why, and the first flight read this way from a sender you haven’t confirmed goes to Review as a suggestion with the fields it found outlined (adding it trusts that sender).
6. **A generic rental car reader** (built). For a rental car whose email has neither markup nor a vendor parser, Waypoint reads labelled lines, table rows or a header row over a row of values (Pick-up and Drop-off, each as a location, a date, a time or all together, the rental company, the car type and a confirmation or reservation number), and needs the word “car”, “rental” or “vehicle” in the message. It needs a pick-up place and a drop-off place (one place is enough when the email says the drop-off is the same, or gives a single pick-up and return location), both dates, and a drop-off after the pick-up and within 90 days. Without times it takes noon and marks the booking as having no time recorded.
7. **A generic train reader** (built). For a train whose email has neither markup nor a vendor parser, Waypoint reads the departure and arrival station, date and time, the operator and a confirmation number, as labelled lines, table rows or two columns, and needs the word “train” (or its equivalent) in a message that doesn’t mention a flight. An outbound and a return in one email give two bookings. It needs both stations, both dates and both times, an arrival after the departure within 96 hours. A train number is used to tell legs apart but isn’t kept, since Waypoint has no field for it yet.
8. **A generic cruise reader** (built). For a cruise whose email has neither markup nor a vendor parser, Waypoint reads the ship, the cruise line, the embarkation and disembarkation port and date (and time, if given) and a booking number. It needs the ship or the line, both ports and both dates, the disembarkation after the embarkation and within 200 days; without times it takes noon to embark and 8:00 to disembark, and marks the booking as having no time recorded. Ports of call are not read.
9. **A “Couldn’t read” review queue** (built). What neither method understands lands in a queue where you can read it in Waypoint, add the booking by hand, ignore the sender (and undo that later from Settings) or dismiss it. Nothing is guessed silently.

## The total on a confirmation or receipt

When a message is read for a booking, Waypoint also looks in it for the booking’s total and, if it finds a clearly labelled one, stores three things on the booking: the amount in the currency’s smallest unit, the currency code, and whether it came from an email or was entered by hand. Nothing else about the total is kept: not the line it was on, the label, or the rest of the message. A follow-up to a booking you can already see (one that names its confirmation code, as described above) can add a missing total in the same way; the follow-up itself is still remembered only by its Gmail id and the booking’s id. Amounts are never converted and nothing is sent anywhere to read them. The cost is visible to exactly the people who see the booking. See [Cost](/Waypoint/start/trips/#cost).

## Languages the generic readers understand

| Reader | English | French | German | Spanish | Japanese |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lodging | yes | no | no | no | yes |
| Flights | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Rental cars | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Trains | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Cruises | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Calendar attachments | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |

For flights that means the words for departure and arrival, booking and confirmation codes, and cancellation text, and dates written as “Nov 21, 2026”, “21 novembre 2026”, “21. November 2026”, “21 de noviembre de 2026”, “21.11.2026”, “2026-11-21” or “2026年11月21日” (a date such as 04/05/2026 that could be read two ways is not guessed), with times as “7:00 PM”, “19:00”, “15h05” or “15時05分”. The words are kept in one table per language in `waypoint/domain/mail/words.py` (flights) and `waypoint/domain/mail/labels.py` (rental cars, trains and cruises, with the confirmation words taken from `words.py`). For rental cars, trains and cruises it means the labels of each field (pick-up and drop-off, departure and arrival stations, embarkation and disembarkation ports, ship, operator or company, confirmation number), the words that say the message is about that kind of booking, and the words that mark a policy, cancellation or “booked on” line, whose dates are ignored; two labels for one field in two languages on one line (“Abholort / Pick-up location: …”) are read. A place’s time zone is taken from an airport code written with it (“Denver International Airport (DEN)”), or from a city name in Waypoint’s airport table: a city in the United States needs its state or country beside it (“Galveston, TX”), a name shared by cities in different time zones is not guessed, and a place Waypoint can’t place is left for you to add by hand, with the reason, rather than given a zone that might be wrong. Stations and ports are not in the table, so a station is placed by the city in its name (“Zurich HB”, “Paris Gare de Lyon”). Whether Waypoint reads a message at all still depends on its subject, sender and snippet (see above), whose booking words are mostly English and Japanese, so a message in another language is read only when its subject or snippet also has one of them (or its sender is a known travel one).

The calendar attachment reader finds the kind of an event from the same words (a flight number and two known airports; “car”, “train”, “cruise” and their French, German, Spanish and Japanese equivalents; “hotel”, “hôtel”, “Unterkunft”, “alojamiento”, “ホテル” and similar for a stay), and reads the confirmation number after any of the confirmation words in the description.

## Optional AI suggestions

Off by default (Settings → Mail and AI → AI; see [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/)). If you turn it on, you can ask a model to suggest the fields of an email in the “Couldn’t read” queue (**Ask AI**), and you confirm or correct the suggestion there. Email text goes to the model only when you press **Ask AI**, unless you also turn on the second, separate switch, **Read unreadable mail with AI automatically** (off by default, with a warning when you turn it on): then a scan also sends the text of travel mail no reader could read, never shopping, codes, newsletters, replies or follow-ups of a booking you can see, through the same module and in the same way as Ask AI, with the same provider settings and the same redaction (or none, with Local) for the mode you chose (see [Reading unreadable mail automatically](/Waypoint/start/ai/#reading-unreadable-mail-automatically)). With either switch off, a scan sends no email text anywhere.

- **Local:** an [Ollama](https://ollama.com) on your own network. The email text goes to it unredacted and untrimmed, and never leaves your network.
- **Hosted:** [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai), restricted to providers with zero data retention (every request denies data collection and requires zero retention).

The suggestion may include the booking's total and its currency, when the email prints them (see [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/#what-comes-back)); that adds two fields to the reply, not to what is sent. Only the plain text goes out. With **Local** it goes exactly as written, so loyalty and Known Traveler numbers in it reach your own Ollama server. With **Hosted** it goes with every email address (the sender’s, the recipient’s, those in a forwarded message’s header lines) replaced by `[email]`, quoted replies and footers removed, and labelled or number-shaped loyalty, Known Traveler and card numbers removed (as well as the numbers saved under Loyalty). Either way the suggestion is a draft for you to confirm, not something Waypoint saves on its own, and prompts and replies are never logged.

The same setting powers one small button that doesn’t involve email at all: the sparkle beside a trip’s destination, which asks the model for a short subtitle such as “Colorado & Texas”. It sends only the trip’s name (without its dates) and the cities, states and countries of its bookings, in order, with the kind of booking: no email text, confirmation code, name, email address, street address or loyalty number, and only when you press it. See [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/#the-destination-button-on-a-trip).

## Addresses are looked up on your server

A booking’s address or place is turned into a rough point on the map (the centre of its ZIP or postal code, an airport or a city) when the booking is saved, so the Stats map can show where you stayed. This is done entirely on your server from lists that ship with Waypoint ([Data sources](/Waypoint/reference/data-sources/)): no geocoding or map service is asked, no request is made, and the address is not sent anywhere. See [Where a booking is on the map](/Waypoint/start/trips/#where-a-booking-is-on-the-map).

## What this means for the rest of the setup

- The Google OAuth client’s ID and secret are set in `.env` (`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`); each connected mailbox’s refresh token is stored encrypted with `WAYPOINT_SECRET_KEY`, and backups hold it encrypted too ([Configuration](/Waypoint/reference/configuration/)).
- Each member connects their own Gmail in Settings and sees only their own connections. A connection ends when its owner can no longer sign in (Waypoint checks hourly, and before every use). Anyone you let in can still download a backup, which holds the encrypted tokens. See [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/AnthonyPluth/waypoint/blob/main/SECURITY.md).

## Live flight status is the one other thing that leaves the server

If you turn on [live flight status](/Waypoint/start/flight-status/), Waypoint asks AeroDataBox (through RapidAPI) about a flight by its number and date. That request carries nothing from your email or your trips beyond the flight number and date: no names, confirmation codes or loyalty numbers. It is off until you set a key.
