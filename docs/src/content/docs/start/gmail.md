Waypoint reads bookings from Gmail with a Google OAuth client that **you** create in your own Google Cloud project, so no one else’s app sits between your mailbox and your server. This page sets that up once for the household; after that, each member connects their own Gmail from **Settings → Mail & review → Gmail**.

Once a Gmail is connected, Waypoint scans it for booking emails: see [Scanning](#scanning) below, and [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/) for what it will and won’t do with a mailbox.

## Create the client

1. In the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/), create a project (any name, such as “Waypoint”).
2. **APIs & Services → Library**: enable the **Gmail API**.
3. **APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen**: choose **External**, give it a name and your email, and add only one scope: `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly`. Waypoint asks for nothing else, so it can’t send, delete, label or change anything.
4. Still on the consent screen, set the publishing status to **In production** (**Publish app**). Leave it unverified: Google doesn’t need to review an app that only your household uses, and each member clicks through its “Google hasn’t verified this app” warning once when connecting.

   :::caution[Don’t leave it in “Testing”]
   While an app is in **Testing**, Google expires its refresh tokens after 7 days, so every Gmail would turn to “Reconnect” weekly. Publish it **In production**.
   :::
5. **APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → OAuth client ID**: choose **Web application**, and add this **authorized redirect URI**, using the address you open Waypoint at (`WAYPOINT_PUBLIC_URL`):

   ```
   https://waypoint.example.com/api/mailboxes/callback
   ```

   Without sign-in, on your own machine, it is the address you open Waypoint at instead, such as `http://localhost:8765/api/mailboxes/callback`.
6. Copy the client ID and secret into `.env` and restart Waypoint:

   ```
   GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID=1234567890-example.apps.googleusercontent.com
   GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET=GOCSPX-example
   ```

Until both are set, Settings says Gmail isn’t set up and shows no Connect button.

## Connect a Gmail

Each member opens **Settings → Mail & review → Gmail** and chooses **Connect Gmail**, picks the Google account and agrees to read-only access. A member can connect more than one address (a personal and a work one, say), and sees only their own connections: nobody else’s address or status appears for them.

- **Reconnect.** If Google stops honouring a connection (you removed Waypoint at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions), the grant expired, or Waypoint’s secret key changed), its row says **Reconnect**. Choosing it connects the same address again.
- **Disconnect.** Waypoint tells Google to revoke its access, then deletes the connection. If Google can’t be reached it keeps the connection and says so, rather than showing it gone while it still works; try again, or remove Waypoint at the Google permissions page.
- **When someone loses access.** A connection ends when the person who made it can no longer sign in to Waypoint (taken off `OIDC_ALLOWED_EMAILS`, or past their sign-in with `OIDC_ALLOWED_GROUPS`): Waypoint checks before every use and once an hour (and whenever anyone opens Settings), revokes the token at Google and deletes it.

## Scanning

Complete bookings are added automatically, even from a sender Waypoint has not seen before. Messages with missing booking details or ambiguous matches stay in Review. Sender trust is not required.

Waypoint scans each connected mailbox in the background, every few hours (by the server’s own clock, `TZ`), and when you choose **Scan now** under the mailbox in Settings. A scan:

1. **Lists on Google’s side** all your mail, newest first, leaving out spam, trash and any sender you chose to ignore (Promotions is included, since booking sites’ mail often lands there). There is no search for words: every message is looked at, so a trip email that never says “confirmation” is still found, whoever sent it. The first scan looks back 18 months; later ones ask Gmail’s history what arrived since the last one ended. **Look back through all mail** (below) is the one search with no date limit.
   Before downloading a message, Waypoint asks Google for only its subject, sender and a short snippet, and decides from those whether it is worth reading. It reads a message whose sender is one of the airlines, hotel groups, rental companies, railways, cruise lines and booking sites it knows, or whose subject or snippet has a travel word (flight, itinerary, e-ticket, boarding pass, check-in, check-out, your stay, hotel, your room, cruise, sailing, rental car, train, 宿泊, チェックイン and the like, or a reservation number together with a place such as an inn or lodge), or that has a general booking word (confirmation, booking, reservation, 予約, 確認) while the sender’s name has a travel word (a hotel group’s or an airline’s). A general booking word on its own isn’t enough, and Gmail’s own categories (Purchases, Reservations) no longer decide anything by themselves, because they also file online orders, restaurant tables and appointments. One case combines them: a message from a sender Waypoint doesn’t know that Gmail files under Purchases or Reservations, with a general booking word, no travel word and nothing retail about it, is read, because a booking confirmation from an unfamiliar site looks like that. If it can’t be made into a booking it goes to Review as one Waypoint couldn’t read (so a restaurant or appointment confirmation under those categories can show up there; dismiss it, or use **Not travel** to ignore the sender). A category with no general booking word, or with a retail word or from a store or carrier Waypoint lists, is still skipped. Shopping mail is skipped: a subject or snippet that is plainly retail (order, shipped, delivered, tracking number, your package, return, refund, receipt, invoice, “thanks for your purchase”) with no travel word, and anything from a short list of online stores and parcel carriers; a sender Waypoint knows as a travel one is never skipped for these, so an airline’s receipt or a hotel’s invoice is still read. It also skips one-time and sign-in codes and “we received your message” auto-replies, and, from a sender it doesn’t know, newsletters and offers with no booking words in the subject and replies (*Re:*), unless the message says it changes or cancels a booking; mail from a sender it knows isn’t skipped for being a reply or an offer. Mail from a bank itself (Chase, Capital One, American Express) is read only when its subject is about travel. A skipped message is remembered by Gmail’s id so it isn’t looked at again, and nothing of it is kept. A scan works for up to 20 minutes. If a big mailbox isn’t finished by then (the first scan of 18 months of mail, or a look-back through all mail, can take a while), the next scan starts straight away and carries on from where it stopped (up to 12 in a row, about four hours; the next scheduled scan carries on after that), and Settings says how many messages it has looked at so far. If Google asks Waypoint to slow down, it waits and tries again.
2. **Reads each match in memory** for the booking markup airlines and hotels add to their emails (schema.org `FlightReservation`, `LodgingReservation`, `RentalCarReservation` and `TrainReservation`, as JSON-LD or microdata), or, for a sender with a parser of its own (Southwest Airlines and Disney Cruise Line so far), for the booking in its text, and keeps the booking’s fields. When the message made a booking, or couldn’t be read and goes to Review, the message itself is also kept, with its images, encrypted, while that holds (see [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/)).
3. **Merges it into your trips.** A booking is one segment. When an email is the same reservation as a booking you can already see, and says nothing new about when or where, it is added to that booking and makes no second one. The same reservation means the same kind and confirmation code, starting within three days of the booking or overlapping its dates; for a flight, also the same airports and flight number (when both give one), and for a train the same stations. Spelling isn’t a difference: an airline, hotel or company written short (“Example” for “Example Air”), a hotel by its brand alone, a rental desk by its airport code or a cruise port by its city is the same, and the booking keeps the wording it had. An email that gives only dates (or a time of midnight for both ends) on the booking’s days is the same, and never wipes its times. The booking shows both emails, newest first, and keeps every field, seat and traveller it had: only what it lacked is filled in, a traveller the new email names who isn’t on it yet is added (so separate emails per traveller for one flight end up on one booking), and nothing you edited or locked changes. A cancelled booking, a different flight number or airport, or a different kind is never taken for the same booking, and neither is a booking you can’t see. The scan’s figures in Settings count these as “emails attached to an existing booking”, a number and nothing else. Otherwise, a later email for the same reservation updates the segment, marking it *changed* (new times, or a car picked up somewhere else) or *cancelled* rather than adding another; an email that only adds times to a booking that had dates alone fills them in without calling it changed, and a field you edited by hand is never overwritten. When the code and dates agree but something contradicts it (a different company, hotel or ship), Waypoint doesn’t guess: an item in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/#match-bookings) asks whether it is that booking or a new one. A segment you added by hand without a code is filled in by an email that gives one, when it is the only such segment you can see with the same kind and place (a stay: the same check-in and check-out days and time zone, and the same name once case, punctuation and words like “the” and “hotel” are set aside; a flight: the same flight number, day and airports); a hand-added segment you can’t see is never touched; the email’s code is then kept on it, and a later email with that code finds it. When more than one hand-added segment you can see fits, Waypoint doesn’t guess: the email is not added, and an item in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/#match-bookings) asks which one it is, or whether it is a new booking. An email with no confirmation code, or about dates more than three days from the segment’s that don’t overlap it, becomes a segment of its own (a cancellation without a code, say), so one booking is never rewritten into another. You can merge or delete the extra one. The booking is yours: you booked it, so you see its trip, and so does anyone it names whom Waypoint can match to a person.
4. **Sends what it can’t read to [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/)**, when it looks like travel. A message from a sender Waypoint doesn’t know that was read, couldn’t be turned into a booking and had only one travel word is not sent to Review: it is remembered by its Gmail id as skipped, with nothing of it kept. Review still gets anything from a sender Waypoint knows, anything in a label you picked, anything with booking markup in it, anything with two or more travel words and the Purchases or Reservations messages with a general booking word described above.

**Look back through all mail** (under the mailbox in Settings) runs the same search over the whole mailbox, with no date limit, instead of just what arrived since the last scan, and reads the messages Waypoint hasn’t read before, newest first. Waypoint asks first, because it reads every message in the mailbox and can take hours for a large inbox: it works in 20-minute scans, each carrying on from where the last stopped (up to 12 in a row, about four hours), and a mailbox too big for that is finished by choosing **Look back through all mail** again, which skips everything already read. The bookings it finds are added as past trips, so a mailbox with years of travel can add many old trips to Review and the trip list. Use it after you add a mailbox’s trips by hand, or when Waypoint starts searching senders it didn’t before: a booking it finds is added when nothing matches, and when it matches a booking you already have (see step 3) it only fills in what that booking lacks (a missing link, seat or terminal, or a traveller), never changing what it already has, its times, its places or its status, so an old email can’t undo a change or a cancellation (for the same reason, a booking from years ago that matches nothing you have is simply added as a past trip). This only protects a booking the email matches: if you moved a booking by more than three days, the old email no longer matches it and is added as a booking of its own, which you can delete. It doesn’t change when the mailbox was last scanned, and messages already read aren’t read again.

You can also pick some of your own Gmail labels to read in full (Settings → Mail & review → Gmail → Choose labels). None are picked by default, Gmail’s own labels such as Inbox and Promotions aren’t offered, and every message in a picked label is read whatever its sender or subject. A label you delete in Gmail drops out of your picks, and the scan’s line says so. A forwarded booking is read as the original email and attributed to the traveller it names; a forward with no original in it goes to Review. Each mailbox in Settings shows when its last scan finished and, in one line, what it came to: how many new messages were found, how many were read, how many bookings were added or changed, how many couldn’t be read (they wait in Review) and how many were skipped (with how many of those were skipped as not travel: shopping and the like), and whether more are waiting; when the AI reads unreadable mail automatically, also how many messages it was asked about in that scan. Only those counts are shown, kept or logged, never a subject, a sender or any text, and they are kept with the mailbox until the next scan replaces them. A look-back or a re-read doesn’t change them. If a scan couldn’t start (the connection needs reconnecting, Google couldn’t be reached for the sign-in, or the mailbox’s owner hasn’t signed in yet), Settings says why under the mailbox (“The scan couldn’t start: …”) until one does; that isn’t counted as a failed scan. A scan that stops halfway (Google couldn’t be reached) keeps what it had read, leaves the last good state as it was, and says what failed there; the next one carries on. Waypoint remembers each message it has read by Gmail’s id, with nothing of its content, so no message is read twice; the one addition is that the record of a message from an ignored sender also keeps that sender’s domain, so stopping the ignore can find it, and stopping the ignore deletes the record. A booking’s times are the clocks at its places. When the markup gives a time with the place’s own offset, or none, that is the time. When the offset isn’t the place’s (senders often print the local time with a UTC mark), Waypoint decides only where it can: a flight is read the one way that gives a believable flight time for the distance between its airports. If both readings fit (a short flight), or it is a stay, a rental or a train, the booking goes to [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/) rather than being filed with a time that may be hours off. A stay or a rental needs a time zone: Waypoint works it out from the city and country in the booking, and when it can’t, the booking goes to Review rather than being given a guess.

### Ignored senders

Under each connected mailbox in Settings → Mail & review → Gmail, **Ignored senders** lists the domains that mailbox ignores (with a count, and “None” when there are none). It shows domains only, never a message, subject or address, and only to the mailbox’s owner. **Stop ignoring** on a domain asks first: Waypoint will read mail from that sender again and look back through what it skipped. Confirming removes the domain, forgets that the mailbox skipped that sender’s messages, and starts a scan straight away, which says “Reading again what was skipped from” the domain. Those messages are read newest first through the same triage as any other mail, and the review items and kept messages that ignoring the sender deleted come back from Gmail, not from a backup, so only mail still in Gmail returns. If a scan of that mailbox is already running, the look-back doesn’t start, and **Look back through all mail** does the same once it finishes. The look-back that **Stop ignoring** starts covers the whole mailbox too, newest first, so it can take a while for a large inbox. Ignoring a sender that’s already ignored, or stopping one that isn’t, changes nothing, and mail whose sender couldn’t be read can’t be ignored.

### Review at a glance

Under each connected mailbox in Settings → Mail & review → Gmail, **Review at a glance** says why mail from that mailbox is waiting in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/) and who it comes from, so you can see which sender is worth a reader of its own or is simply not travel. It shows counts, sender domains and a few fixed phrases, never a message, subject, address, confirmation code or any text from a message, and only to the mailbox’s owner (a mailbox shared with the household shows it to its owner alone). It has four parts:

- **Why**: the number of items waiting, then each reason with how many are waiting and how many of those were added in the last 30 days: no booking data found, dates or place missing, couldn’t be opened, could be one of your bookings, and read by the lodging reader and needing a check.
- **What’s missing**: for the items with dates or place missing, how many lack a place, a start time, an end time, times with their dates, a time that can be read, or something else. Waypoint records one of these fixed labels on the item when the scan puts it in Review, so items that were already waiting before this was added aren’t counted here.
- **Senders with the most waiting**: the ten domains with the most items, each with its count and its commonest reason. The domain links to Review showing only that sender’s mail in that mailbox (**Show everything** clears it), and **Ignore this sender** asks first, exactly as in Review, and can be undone under Ignored senders or with the Undo that appears.
- **Added to Review per scan**: for each of the last ten scans in the last 30 days that added any, how many of its items are still waiting. Dismissing or adding an item takes it out of its scan’s count, and a scan that added nothing isn’t listed.

### Times marked UTC

Some airlines write a flight’s times as UTC (`…T09:00:00Z`) when they mean the airport’s own clock. Waypoint settles which from the message itself, in memory: it looks for both readings of the times (as written, and moved to the airport’s zone) among the times of day the email shows (`9:00 AM`, `9:00am`, `09:00`, `9:00`). If only the as-written times appear, they are the local times; if only the moved ones appear, the markup really was UTC; the same reading applies to the departure and the arrival together. If both or neither appear, Waypoint keeps the move and the booking’s card says **Check the times** until you edit or confirm them (saving the times, even unchanged, confirms them). A time with a real offset (`-06:00`) is never second-guessed. Only the times of day are kept from the text, and only while the message is read.

What Waypoint can’t do yet: read mail with no booking markup from a sender that has no parser yet (they come one vendor at a time; the rest goes to Review with a reason, so a booking in a shape Waypoint doesn’t know shows up there instead of nowhere). Cruise lines don’t put booking markup in their emails, so their mail is found by the search but goes to Review: with [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/) on, it comes with a suggested cruise (ports, dates and time zones) to check and add; without them, you add the cruise by hand.

## What Waypoint keeps

Per connection: the Gmail address, a **refresh token** and where its scans had got to (the point in Gmail’s history, and when the last scan finished or what stopped it), plus the ids of the messages already read (for one from an ignored sender, also the sender’s domain, deleted when you stop ignoring it) and the [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/) items. The refresh token is encrypted with `WAYPOINT_SECRET_KEY` (in backups too), is decrypted only to refresh or revoke it, and never appears in logs or error pages. Access tokens are used for the one call that needs them and aren’t kept. A backup holds the connections, so restoring one needs the same key; otherwise the connections turn to **Reconnect**.

The **Ignored senders** list starts collapsed. Expand its count to manage individual senders.
