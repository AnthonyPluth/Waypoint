A **trip** is a journey. It holds **segments**: one flight leg, hotel stay, car rental, train, cruise or parking reservation each. You can add them by hand, and Waypoint adds them from the booking emails in a connected Gmail (see [Scanning](/Waypoint/start/gmail/#scanning)).

## The pages

- **Upcoming** leads with a pass card for what is next: a line at the top that counts down (below), the confirmation code as the largest thing on the card, which you tap to copy, then the flight number, date and departure time, the airports with a route line and the time at each, and below the tear line the terminal, seat and cabin and the booking’s status (a hotel’s card leads with its check-in and check-out and its city and state). A flight booked on two reservations shows each code. A flight in the air or a rental car out counts as under way and leads until it ends; a hotel stay in progress doesn’t push the day’s flight aside. Below it, the trip that card belongs to (or the next one, when you aren’t travelling) is laid out day by day. On a day you leave, the list puts the hotel check-out first, then the car’s return, then the departure, whatever hours the bookings show (a check-out time is often the hotel’s standard hour, not when you walk out). Each booking in that list, and the card’s **Open** button, links to that booking’s card on its trip page, which scrolls to it and outlines it; clicking one opens its full card in a sheet (it slides up from the bottom on a phone and is a side panel on a larger screen), with an **Open trip** button to go to the trip page, and Ctrl- or Cmd-click still follows the link. In that day-by-day list a flight leaves out its cabin, a hotel with a known brand shows the brand (Hyatt Place) and any other hotel its full name, each with its city and state from the address (City, State when both are known, the city alone or the state alone when only one is), and a hotel’s full name appears when you hover over a branded one. An address that can’t be read is shown as it was written. The trip page keeps the full names, cabins and addresses. Cancelled segments never lead.
  - The line at the top of a flight’s card is worded by what Waypoint can vouch for. With no live flight status (it is off, has nothing for the flight, is more than 3 hours old, or this device is offline) it counts from the booked times and says so: “Check-in usually opens in …”, then from 24 hours before departure (the same 24 hours as the [check-in reminder](/Waypoint/start/reminders/), an estimate rather than the airline’s own rule) “Check-in usually open, scheduled departure in …”, then once the booked departure has passed “Scheduled arrival in …”, then “Scheduled arrival time passed”. It never says “Under way” or “Landed” from the clock alone. Fresh live status takes over only where it knows: “Delayed 50 min, expected to leave in …”, “Departed, expected to arrive in …”, “Landed”, “Cancelled by the airline” or “Diverted”. A stay, car, train, cruise or parking booking keeps its own words (“Check-in in …”, “Pick-up in …”, “Drop-off in …”, “Entry in …”, “Exit in …”). The plane on the route line is placed from the booked departure and arrival and carries a small “Based on schedule” label, so a delay is never implied; it moves to the end only when live status says the flight landed, and stays at the start for a cancelled one. The booked times themselves are never changed. On Upcoming, a flight in the air by the schedule is marked “In the air, by schedule”.
- **Trips** lists the trips you can see as cards: the ones still to come or under way, and the past ones. Each card shows the trip’s name, dates and destination, who is travelling and small icons for what it holds: a plane for flights, a building for stays, a car, a train, a ship or a parking sign (cancelled bookings don’t count). On a computer the list sits beside the selected trip in two panes: pick a card to see that trip without leaving the page. On a phone or tablet the list stays a list, and a card opens the trip’s page. Pick a traveller to see only their trips; the choice is kept in the address (`#trips?who=…`) so you can bookmark it.
- A **trip** page lays the trip out as a timeline of pass cards, one per booking in the order of the trip. Each card has the same top line as on Upcoming (counting from the booked times), the confirmation code to tap and copy, the airports and a route line for a flight, its local times, the terminal, seat and cabin, and the booking’s status. The trip’s travellers are listed once under its title and dates: everyone on any booking, each once, in the order they first appear (left out when you are the only one on the trip). Below the tear line a flight lists its travellers with their seats (a flight on two reservations is one card with a block for each code). A booking’s card shows its times as they are at the place and doesn’t print the time zone’s name; the zone is a field in the booking’s edit form. A hotel, rental car or parking booking never lists its travellers; the trip’s line at the top says who is on the trip. A cruise lists its travellers like a flight. A train lists its travellers only when they aren’t simply the trip’s whole set or when one has a seat. For a hotel, a rental car or a cruise, a **Loyalty** line shows the program and masked number of each traveller who has one for that chain or cruise line, one per line and without names (a booking where nobody has one shows no such block). Tap a number to show it and copy it; tap again to hide it. A flight shows no frequent-flyer number, since it was used when the flight was booked; the household’s numbers are on [People](/Waypoint/start/people/). A traveller known only by their printed name is marked as not matched to a person yet on a flight. Waypoint tells the program from the provider’s name (“American Airlines” is American AAdvantage), so write the provider the way the airline or chain does.

Add a booking from Trips (Waypoint puts it in the trip it belongs to, or starts one) or from a trip page (it goes in that trip), edit any segment, or remove it. On a computer, **Edit** opens the form right under the booking you chose, scrolled into view wherever you are on the page. On a phone, **Edit** opens the form in a sheet that slides up from the bottom over the dimmed page; **Save** or **Cancel** closes it and leaves you where you were on the page, and so do swiping the sheet down, tapping outside it, pressing Escape and the close button. The pencil beside a trip’s name renames it, and **Delete trip** removes the whole trip and every booking on it, after a confirmation that says how many bookings go with it. If you changed a booking or a trip’s name, closing it any way (Cancel, the close button, tapping outside, Escape, swiping down), starting another edit, picking another trip, using the browser’s Back button or closing the tab first asks **Discard changes?**; **Keep editing** returns to what you typed, and nothing closes a form while it is saving. A save that fails keeps everything you typed and says why, inside the sheet on a phone; a page that can’t reload its trips shows an error with Try again, not the old trips as if they were current.

## The destination under a trip’s dates

The line under a trip’s name shows its dates and, after a dot, its **destination** (“Colorado & Texas”). Waypoint writes it for you from the places of the trip’s bookings, in the order of the trip, and keeps it up to date whenever you add, edit, move, merge, split or remove a booking, or an email changes one. A flight or train counts where it arrives and a stay by the state or country of its address (or its city, when it has one), and a rental car by its address or pick-up airport. Cancelled bookings don’t count, and neither does the place you set out from. In the United States a place is named by its state (Colorado), anywhere else by its country (Japan), and a place Waypoint has no region for by the city as it was written. Up to four places are listed (“Colorado, Texas & Utah”); a longer trip shows three and counts the rest (“Colorado, Texas, Utah & 2 more”). All of this is worked out from reference lists inside Waypoint: nothing is looked up or sent anywhere.

The pencil beside the dates opens a **Destination** field. Type your own and Waypoint keeps exactly what you wrote, whatever happens to the bookings later, including when you merge the trip with another or move a booking out of it. Clear the field and save to hand it back to Waypoint, which fills it in again at once. A trip that already had a destination when this arrived keeps it as written if it isn’t what Waypoint would have chosen when the trip was made.

When the household has turned on the optional [AI fallback](/Waypoint/start/ai/), a small sparkle button next to the field asks the AI for a short destination. It sends only the trip’s name (without its dates) and the cities, states and countries of its bookings in order, with the kind of each booking: never a date, a confirmation code, a traveller’s name, an email address, a street address, a loyalty number or any email text. The answer fills the field for you to change or accept, and nothing is saved until you press **Save**; saving it counts as writing it yourself. The button isn’t there while the AI fallback is off, and an AI assistant connected over MCP can’t use it.

## Times beside yours

A segment’s time is always shown as it is at the place, and nothing is converted to or stored in your own time zone.

## Who sees a trip

You see a trip when you are on it (its people list, which always includes everyone named on any of its bookings: see [The people on a trip](#the-people-on-a-trip)), when you booked it (the trip, or any of its segments), or when the confirmation of one of its bookings came to your own connected mailbox (see [One booking, one segment](#one-booking-one-segment)). Nobody else sees it, however they ask: its address answers “not found”, the same as a trip that doesn’t exist. A solo work trip stays its traveller’s. If you book a trip for a guest (a child, a grandparent), you see it because you booked it. Add someone to a trip, or as a traveller on a segment, and the whole trip is shared with them. Someone’s access to a trip ends only when they are taken off the trip’s people, not when they are taken off a booking.

Without sign-in, on your own machine, everyone is the one local household, which sees every trip.

## Segments

Each segment has a kind (flight, hotel, car, train, cruise or parking), a status (confirmed, changed or cancelled), a confirmation code, the provider, a start and an end, where it starts and ends, a link to manage the booking, and details: flight number, terminal, seat, cabin, aircraft, room, car class, address, phone, and for parking the space type, the vehicle and notes (each up to 200 characters). A rental car has one place field for pick-up: the form asks for the **Pick-up place** and has no separate address, because that text is the address Directions, the time zone lookup, the map and the calendar’s location use. Saving a car sets its address from the pick-up place when it has none or when it was the old pick-up place. An address that differs from the pick-up place (an email gave the branch name and a street address) is kept: the form then also shows a **Pick-up address** field you can change or clear (cleared, the address follows the pick-up place again), and the card shows a **Pick-up address** line; a car whose address matches its pick-up place has neither. An empty **Drop-off place** (the field says “Same as pick-up”) is saved as the pick-up place; a drop-off somewhere else is kept. The **travellers** of a flight, train or cruise are people from [People](/Waypoint/start/people/), or, until a name on a booking is matched to a person, the name as printed. A hotel, rental car or parking booking never asks who is on it: it is for everyone on its trip (see below).

A segment is added to a trip you name, or without one: Waypoint puts it in the trip it belongs to (see below) or makes a new trip. Flights from before Waypoint can be [imported from another app’s CSV export](/Waypoint/start/import/).

## Cost

A booking can carry its **cost**: the total for that booking, as a whole amount in the currency’s smallest unit with the currency’s three-letter code (`USD`, `EUR`, `JPY`). Waypoint never converts between currencies and makes no outside call to do it, so a trip’s total is shown per currency, such as **Total $1,240.00 + €310.00**, beneath the trip’s dates. When some of a trip’s bookings have no cost the total says so (**3 of 5 bookings**); the count treats one confirmation code as one booking, so a round trip whose total is on its outbound flight counts as priced.

- **From an email.** When Waypoint reads a confirmation or receipt it also looks for the total. It takes an amount only when its line is labelled as the total: *Total*, *Grand total*, *Total charged*, *Total paid*, *Total due*, *Amount charged*, *Amount paid*, *Order total* or *Estimated total* (in English), with a currency symbol or code beside it (`$1,234.56` and `1.234,56 €` both read). Subtotals, taxes and fees on their own, deposits, nightly or per-person rates, points and miles, refunds and negative amounts are never taken, and an amount with no currency is left alone. When an email has two different totals, or the amount is unclear, the cost is left empty rather than guessed. A booking’s schema.org markup (`totalPrice` with `priceCurrency`) is read the same way. A total that covers a round trip or several bookings under one confirmation code goes on the first of them; an email with bookings under different codes gives none of them a cost. A cancelled booking gets no cost from the email.
- **By hand.** The booking’s edit form has a **Cost** field (the amount and the currency). Change it and the cost is yours: a later email never replaces it. Clear the amount to remove the cost. A cost you leave alone stays as it was read.
- **Later emails.** A receipt or other follow-up about a booking fills a missing cost and never changes one that is already there (a follow-up whose code is shared by several legs of one booking fills none, since its total can’t be put on one leg). A second confirmation for the same booking attaches to it the same way (it only fills). An email that changes the booking (its times or places moved) also updates a cost that was read from an email, to the new total; one you entered by hand is never touched, and an email with no total never clears a cost.
- **Cancelled bookings** keep their cost on the card, struck through and marked as not counted, and are left out of the trip’s total.
- **Who sees it.** A cost is part of its booking: everyone who can see the booking and its trip sees it, and no one else does. It is part of the [offline copy](/Waypoint/start/offline/) of a current trip.

## Seats

On a flight or train, each traveller has a seat of their own: tick them in the booking’s edit form and a **Seat** field appears under their name (up to 10 characters, as it reads on the boarding pass). The card shows it beside their name, and the calendar event lists the seats by traveller. A seat counts as an edit of the travellers, so a later email won’t change it. Leaving a seat out when changing who is on a booking keeps it. A seat entered on a booking before this (one for all of them) stays on the booking, and moves to the traveller when the booking has only one and is edited.

## Actions on a booking

Each booking card has buttons for what you'd do next:

- **Open in app**: the booking's manage link (from the email, or one you entered). It's a link on the provider's own website; on an iPhone or Android phone it says **Open in app** and the provider's app opens it if installed; on a computer it says **Manage booking** and opens the provider's website. Only https links are offered. A cancelled booking keeps this one (you may need it for a refund) and loses the others. On a flight, from 24 hours before it departs (the same moment as the check-in reminder) until it departs, the button says **Check in** and opens the same link, where the airline's app or site checks you in and shows your boarding pass; Waypoint can't link to the pass itself or make a Wallet pass. A flight whose email gave no manage link opens the airline's own website instead (on a phone, its app if installed), where you look the booking up with its confirmation code, which the card shows and copies with a tap: United and Delta (My Trips), Alaska (find a reservation), American (find your reservation) and Southwest (check in). The email's manage link always comes first, since it belongs to that booking. These addresses are plain pages of the airline's site: nothing from your booking is in them, and Waypoint never requests them, your device does when you tap.
- **Address**: a hotel's address (a rental car's, when it differs from its pick-up place, as **Pick-up address**) shows under its times, as written (up to 300 characters, over several lines if you like). Tap it to copy. With none, the card says **Add address**, which opens the edit form at that field. Editing a booking's details (address, room, phone and the rest) keeps them as a set: a later email won't replace them, but it fills the address while you haven't edited any of them. An address over 300 characters in an email is shortened to fit. Waypoint never looks addresses up; it has only what an email or you gave it, and the calendar feed carries it as the event's location.
- **Parking**: a reserved parking spot (an airport lot, a cruise-port garage, a hotel or event lot). Its place is the lot’s name, the operator is its provider, and the address (for Directions) and the entry and exit are wall-clock times at the lot, in the lot’s one time zone, which Waypoint works out from the address when you leave it empty. The details are the space type (covered, economy, valet), the vehicle (make and colour, never a licence plate) and notes. The card says Entry and Exit; the day-by-day list shows the booking on the day it starts and again on the day it ends; a Directions button opens the address; and while the vehicle is parked the booking counts as under way, like a rental car. Your calendar feed holds it as one event from entry to exit, with the address as its location. It has no “Open in app” button of its own yet. When its email carries the gate’s QR code, the booking can show it full-screen: see [The gate code of a parking booking](#the-gate-code-of-a-parking-booking). Waypoint doesn’t read parking confirmations by itself yet: add one by hand, or from a suggestion in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/).
- **Ports of call**: a cruise keeps the ports the ship calls at, in order, each with its name, its own time zone and the local times the ship arrives and leaves (either, both or neither). Add, move and remove them in the booking’s edit form; the card shows the sailing day by day: a numbered row for each date with the port and the local times the ship arrives and leaves (the first day is the embarking port, the last the disembarking one), and “Sea day” for a date with no port call, the same days the [Stats](/Waypoint/start/stats/) page counts. The list is checked as you save: a departure can’t be before the arrival, and the ports must follow one another between embarking and disembarking. Editing the list keeps it like any other edit: a later email won’t replace it, and fills it only while it is empty. The terminal’s address works like a hotel’s, and the calendar feed carries the sailing as one event with the itinerary in its description.
- **Directions**: an Apple Maps link to a hotel's or rental's address (or its place name when there's no address). Your device hands the text to Apple Maps when you tap it; nothing is sent from the server.
- **Call**: dials the phone number on the booking, when it has one.

## The gate code of a parking booking

A parking reservation’s email carries the QR code that opens the gate, sometimes only in a separate “QR code reminder” sent after the confirmation. Waypoint keeps that picture with the booking and shows it large from the booking, so it is one tap away instead of buried in your mailbox at the garage.

- **Where it comes from.** From the pictures of the booking’s kept emails (see [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/)). Waypoint can’t tell for sure that a picture is a QR code without reading it, and it never does, so it picks by a fixed rule: a PNG or JPEG between 100 and 1,000 pixels a side, within 10% of square and not wider than tall, that is not a picture the message shows twice (a header or logo). A logo, a wide banner and a tracking pixel never qualify. When exactly one picture of a message qualifies it is kept as the booking’s code. When several qualify none is picked, and the booking offers **Choose the code**: a sheet lists the emails’ pictures and the one you tap becomes the code. A booking whose emails have no qualifying picture shows nothing. A booking that shows a code also offers **Choose another picture**, in case Waypoint picked a logo.
- **From a later email.** The code can arrive in a reminder after the confirmation. When a follow-up email about a parking booking (matched by its confirmation code, as described under [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/)) holds a qualifying picture, that picture alone is kept with the booking, and nothing else of the email: no text, subject or markup. A code from a newer email replaces an older one; an older email never replaces a newer code. The booking says which email its code came from by day only.
- **Next up and Also under way.** The card at the top of Upcoming is a flight, train or cruise that is under way, otherwise whatever starts soonest. A hotel stay, rental car or parking booking you are in the middle of never takes the card from a departure that is still ahead: it is listed under **Also under way** with its check-out, drop-off or exit time, and opens like any other booking.
- **Show QR code.** On the booking (the trip page, and the Next up card on Upcoming when it is the next thing) the **Show QR code** button opens a full-screen view with the picture centred on a white square with a clear margin around it, as large as the screen allows, with sharp pixels (never smoothed), and the lot’s name and the entry and exit times under it. It asks the browser to keep the screen on while it is open, where the browser can, and reminds you to turn the screen brightness up. **Close** and Escape close it, and focus goes back to the button. The picture is shown exactly as it was received: Waypoint doesn’t decode, copy or print the code’s contents. Once the booking’s exit time has passed, or the booking is cancelled, the button is gone, but the picture stays as long as the email does.
- **Online only.** The picture is fetched from your server each time you open the view and is never saved in the browser, so it needs a connection. It is not part of the [offline copy](/Waypoint/start/offline/) of a trip. Whether to allow an offline copy of a gate credential is a decision for the household’s owner.
- **Who can see it.** Exactly the people who can see the booking: its travellers, whoever booked it and whoever received its confirmation, through a route that answers 404 to anyone else. It is never in the calendar feed, a reminder, a notification, a log, the offline copy, a backup in the clear, the scan’s summary or what an AI assistant over [MCP](/Waypoint/start/mcp/) can read (an assistant can’t reach the picture, and the booking it reads doesn’t say there is one).
- **Kept like the email.** It is encrypted with the key that protects the kept emails, in backups too. It goes with the booking, with the mailbox it came from, and when you tap **Done** on a trip to remove its saved emails. Editing the booking never removes it.

## Past trips you only remember

For a trip from long ago, when you know the country and the dates but not the airports or hotels, choose **Add a past trip** on Trips. You pick a country, the first and last day, and who travelled. For the United States you can also pick a state (in each country row, if there are several): it names the trip and its destination after the state (Alaska rather than United States), sets that visit’s time zone from it, and fills that state on the [Stats](/Waypoint/start/stats/) map; leave it as **Any state** and no state is filled. Waypoint makes it a trip like any other (named for the country and month), so you can open it later and add the flights, stays or rentals as you find them. A past trip is a **visit** booking: it has no times, place or cost and no reminders. Your calendar feed lists it by its country. The country, its first-visit date and the dates feed the [Stats](/Waypoint/start/stats/) countries and map and the year picker, and nothing else: a visit adds no flights, distance or nights away. A flight or stay you add to the same trip later joins it, and a country is counted once however many bookings name it. It is visible to the people on it, like any trip. Its card shows just its From and To dates, with the year, and no “time not recorded” line; a booking of another kind with no times shows the day of each end beside “time not recorded”, and a date outside this year shows its year.

A trip that went through more than one country takes them all in one go: under the first country, **Add another country** adds a row (country, first day, last day) for as many as 20, each with its own **Remove**. A new row starts on the previous row's last day, so you only change what differs, and who travelled is chosen once. **Add** then makes one trip with a visit for each country. The same button is on a trip's page under **Add a booking** → **Past trip**, which adds the countries to that trip. Each country keeps its own days: they may overlap (a border-crossing day, or a split you don't remember), and the same country may appear twice (out and back). The visits are shown in date order, each is edited or removed on its own, and each is its own event in your calendar feed ("Visit: Ecuador", "Visit: Peru").

A trip made from several countries is named for them in date order, like “Trip to Ecuador and Peru (Mar 2005)”, “Trip to Spain, France and Italy (Jun 2008)” or, past three, “Trip to Spain, France, Italy and 2 more (Jun 2008)”, and its destination lists them the same way. Adding a country to a trip that still has its automatic name renames it; a name or destination you set yourself is never changed. The trip's dates run from the earliest first day to the latest last day. When a past trip has only countries, its [route replay](#replaying-a-past-trips-route) visits them in date order once it has two or more.

## Done with a trip

Waypoint keeps the original email of a booking only while something needs it (see [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/)), and nothing deletes it by itself when a trip is over. Once a trip has ended at least 7 days ago and Waypoint holds saved emails for its bookings that can be removed, its page shows **Done**: tap it, check the trip looks right, and confirm **Remove emails**. The saved emails for that trip’s bookings are deleted, their images with them, and a message tells you how many. The bookings stay exactly as they are, and this can’t be undone. An email that something else still needs stays: one that also made a booking in another trip, or one still waiting in [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/). Anyone who can see the trip can do this, and no AI assistant can. A trip that ended less than 7 days ago shows no Done button, and once nothing is left to remove (every email still held is one something else needs) the button is gone; the count it shows is only the emails it would remove. Pressing **Done** also marks the trip as done, and Waypoint remembers when: a done trip’s booking cards no longer show Edit, Move or Remove, while a past trip nobody has marked done keeps them. Waypoint saves that mark with the trip, so it is in a backup and in the copy saved for [offline access](/Waypoint/start/offline/); it hides the buttons on the trip’s page and doesn’t lock anything else (the booking can still be changed from an AI assistant with write access or by moving another booking into the trip), and it can’t be undone from the app. The mark is set whenever a member presses Done on an ended trip, including one with nothing left to remove (the page just doesn’t offer the button then), and pressing it again doesn’t change when it was set. The confirmation says that Done hides Edit, Move and Remove.

## Times are where they happen

A segment’s times are the wall-clock times at its places, stored with the place’s time zone (an IANA name such as `Pacific/Auckland`). Waypoint never converts them to the server’s time zone or to UTC: a 22:15 departure from Auckland is 22:15 in Auckland, and the arrival that is the same day in Los Angeles is shown at its own local time. Type a time as it appears on the booking, like `2026-03-01T22:15`, without an offset. A flight’s zones come from its airports (Waypoint knows the airports by their three-letter code); name the zone yourself for a place that isn’t on the list. A hotel stay is in one place, so it has **one time zone** for check-in and check-out: type it, or leave it empty and Waypoint works it out from the address. It reads the address text itself, offline (nothing is looked up or sent anywhere): a US state with its ZIP code, or a state in an address that says it is in the US (a bare “DE” or “CO” is never taken for Delaware or Colorado, since it may be Germany or Colombia), down to the ZIP prefix in states that span zones, such as Florida’s panhandle or El Paso), a Canadian province with a Canadian postal code or Canada named (for Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and the Atlantic provinces; British Columbia, Ontario, Quebec and Newfoundland have parts in another zone, so there a postal code outside those parts, or a city Waypoint knows, decides), a country with a single zone, or a city it knows. A state and ZIP code with no country named are taken only when they agree (so “Berlin, DE 10115” is never Delaware), and a province or ZIP prefix that mixes zones (northern Ontario, the East Kootenay, a few Kentucky and Tennessee ZIP prefixes) is left to a city Waypoint knows. When the address doesn’t settle it, Waypoint says so and you type the zone. The stay’s card shows the zone it uses, so a wrong guess is easy to spot and fix; an older stay saved with two different zones takes its start zone for both the next time it is edited. Confirmation emails get the same treatment when they give an address but no city it knows. Waypoint refuses a segment that ends before it starts, comparing the two at their own zones, so a flight across the date line, whose arrival is earlier on the clock than its departure, is fine.

Every time zone field (a booking’s start and end, a cruise port’s, and the same fields when you add a booking from [Review](/Waypoint/start/review/)) is a search box: type a city (`Chicago`), a region (`Europe`), a US state or Canadian province, a common name (`Eastern`, `Pacific`, `EST`, `GMT`) or a country (`Japan`, `India`), in any case and with or without spaces, and pick from the list under it. Arrow keys move through the matches, Enter or Tab picks one and Escape puts back what was there. The field’s empty choice (such as “Work it out from the address”) is the first entry while it is empty and becomes **Clear** once it has a zone. Only a zone from the list can be saved, and the saved value is still the IANA name; a browser that can’t list zones shows a plain text field for the name instead.

A trip’s dates are the local dates of its first and last segments.

## Where a booking is on the map

When a stay, car, train, cruise or parking booking is saved (added by hand, from an email or an import, edited, or updated by a later email), Waypoint works out roughly where it is and stores that point with the booking, so the [Stats map](/Waypoint/start/stats/#the-map) can show it. Nothing is looked up when a page is opened, and nothing is sent anywhere: the address and place names are read on your server against lists that ship with Waypoint ([Data sources](/Waypoint/reference/data-sources/)), with no map or geocoding service.

The point is never a street address. It is, in order of preference:

- **ZIP or postal code**: the centre of the ZIP code (US) or postal code area (the first part of a Canadian or British postcode) in the booking’s address.
- **Airport**: for a car or train, the airport named by its pick-up or arrival place (“SFO” or “SFO airport”).
- **City**: the city the booking names, or the one in its address, when it is clear which one is meant: a name shared by several places (Springfield) needs a state or country in the address, or the place is left off the map rather than guessed.

Flights have no stored point (their airports already have coordinates). A booking whose place can’t be read has no point and isn’t drawn. Editing a booking’s address or place works the point out again; moving a booking to another trip, merging and splitting leave it as it is. The point is kept in a [backup](#in-a-backup), is not shown on a booking’s card or in the API’s booking replies, and bookings that were saved before this existed were located once when Waypoint was updated, from the same lists.

## How segments are grouped

Without a trip named, a new segment joins an existing grouped trip when it is within two days of it, the trip hasn’t already got back to where it began before the segment starts, and everyone the segment involves (its travellers and whoever booked it) is already on the trip. Otherwise it starts a trip of its own, so one person’s solo trip never takes in another person’s booking. You can **rename** a trip, **merge** two of yours into one and **split** some segments off into a new one; a trip you merged or split is yours from then on, and grouping leaves it alone. 

On a trip’s page, **Merge another trip** brings another trip’s bookings into this one, and each booking has a **Move** button to send it to another trip or to a new trip of its own (not offered for a trip’s only booking). A trip left with no bookings is removed. A booking can move into any trip you can see, even one with different people. When the people differ, Waypoint first tells you who would newly see the booking (the people who see that trip: those on it, whoever booked it and whoever got the confirmation for one of its bookings in their own mailbox) and who would newly see that trip (the booking’s travellers, whoever booked it and whoever got its confirmation in their own mailbox), leaving out anyone who already sees what they would gain, and moves it only when you choose **Move anyway**. An AI assistant can’t confirm such a move at all: it sends you to the app to do it, while a move into a trip with the same people still works for it. The sheet lists your other trips newest first. **Merge another trip** works the same way: when the two trips involve different people, Waypoint first tells you who would newly see the bookings of this trip (the people only on the other trip) and who would newly see the other trip’s bookings (the people only on this one), and merges them only when you choose **Merge anyway**. The merged trip’s people are everyone on either trip. An AI assistant can’t confirm such a merge: it sends you to the app to do it, while a merge of trips with the same people still works for it.

## The people on a trip

Besides the travellers on each booking, a trip keeps a list of the people on it. It always includes everyone named on any of its bookings, and it can hold more: the people a road trip is for before anything is booked. A trip made by hand (through an AI assistant, until the app has a form for it) can be given its people, and its people can be changed later: adding someone who can’t see the trip yet needs a confirmation that an AI assistant can’t give, taking someone off removes them from the trip’s stays and rentals, and is refused while they are on one of its flights (take them off the flight first). Merging, splitting and moving bookings carry the people along, and claiming a guest replaces them with the member on every trip. A hotel, rental car or parking booking, added by hand or made from an email, is for everyone on the trip, so it follows the list instead of naming the lead guest (flights and cruises keep the travellers the email names, and a flight its seats). The form for these has no traveller choice, and a list sent by an AI assistant or another client is ignored; editing one keeps it that way, moving it into a trip of other people asks first and then gives it the group it had, and ones added before this keep the travellers they have. A guest name on a hotel, car or parking email that isn’t matched to a person in People is not kept or shown: the booking is simply for everyone on the trip. If the email names someone who couldn’t otherwise see the trip, that booking keeps its own list so nobody’s access changes. Splitting a trip takes the people on the bookings that moved, and takes off the original trip anyone who is now on none of its bookings (a flight counts), unless a booking for everyone is left there. Everyone on the list sees the trip and all its bookings, so adding someone changes who sees the trip, which is why it asks first.

## One flight, one card

When the same flight is booked on two reservations (a family split across two confirmation codes), each booking stays its own segment, so its change and cancellation emails, its manage link and your edits land on the right one. Waypoint shows them together: **one card per flight** (the same flight number, local departure date and airports) with the route, times and live status once, then a **Bookings** section with a block for each confirmation code: the code to tap and copy, its travellers, and its own Edit and Remove. If the bookings disagree on the times (one was changed and the other not yet), the card says **Times differ between bookings** and shows each booking’s times in its block instead of picking one. Upcoming shows the flight once, with every code. The calendar feed has one event for the flight, listing every booking’s code, and the check-in and day-of reminders and the flight-status check count it once. Hotels and rental cars are never grouped.

## One booking, one segment

A confirmation email that arrives twice (in one mailbox, or in two household members’ mailboxes) makes one segment, not two. Waypoint matches a booking across the whole household by its kind, confirmation code (ignoring case and spaces), places and date, and its flight number however it is written (`AA 4001`, `AA4001` and `AA04001` are one flight) and the provider after tidying its name (“American Airlines Inc.” and “American Airlines” are the same). A different confirmation code on the same flight is another booking. When the email came to someone who isn’t on the booking’s trip, they are noted as having received its confirmation and see that trip: the email is already in their own mailbox, but they then see the whole trip it belongs to (every segment and traveller on it), and the same match lets their email update or cancel that booking. Nobody else gains access.

## What your edits keep

When you change a field of a segment, Waypoint remembers it was you and a later email never overwrites it. An email about a booking Waypoint already has updates that segment instead of adding another, and marks it *changed* when its times or places moved, or *cancelled* when the airline says so.

## Live status

With a RapidAPI key set, a flight’s card also shows its live status (delays, gate, terminal) beside the booked times, which it never changes. See [Live flight status](/Waypoint/start/flight-status/).

With a Logo.dev key saved in Settings, each booking also shows its airline’s, hotel’s, rental company’s or cruise line’s logo. See [Brand logos](/Waypoint/start/logos/).

## Past trips

Once a trip has ended (its last day is before today, the same test the Trips list uses for its Past group), its page shows condensed booking cards: the kind, title or provider, dates and times, route or place, address, confirmation number and travellers stay, and what only helps while you travel goes: the buttons that open the reservation in its app or the vendor’s site, Directions, Call, View email, Add address, live flight status and loyalty numbers. Edit, Move and Remove stay until the trip is marked Done (see [Done with a trip](#done-with-a-trip)). Upcoming and current trips keep their full cards.

## Replaying a past trip's route

A trip that has ended and has at least two stops with a known place shows its route at the top of the trip, and plays it once when the page opens. Upcoming and current trips keep their normal page. The map draws the path in time order: every flight, the one back included, is its own arc with a small plane flying along it in the direction of travel (the icon disappears once it lands), and a stay is its own diamond marker (a car, a train or a cruise's ports count when Waypoint knows the place) even when it is next to an airport. When a flight lands, the camera zooms smoothly into the area around its destination and a small car travels from the airport to the next stays and pickups there, in time order; a cruise is a boat travelling from the port of departure through the ports of call. Then the camera zooms back out for the next flight. No map tiles are fetched, and the zoomed land is the same coarse outline as the whole map. When two stays or pickups on the road are at least 50 miles apart, the car follows the US interstates between them: Waypoint snaps each end to the nearest interstate (within 30 miles), follows the shortest way along the interstate network, and joins each end with a short straight line. The roads near that route are drawn faintly behind it with a few route shields (like I-80), only where the map is close enough to read them. Faint dashed state lines are drawn behind the roads the same way, under every flight and drive of the trip, wherever the map shows the United States closely enough to read them (not on a map of the whole world, and only those in view), from another list that ships with Waypoint ([Data sources](/Waypoint/reference/data-sources/#state-lines)); they are only the lines between two states, since the coast and the borders with Canada and Mexico are already part of the land outline, and they carry no names. This is a coarse picture, not turn-by-turn directions, and not the road you actually drove: the interstates come from a list that ships with Waypoint ([Data sources](/Waypoint/reference/data-sources/#interstates)), nothing is looked up outside your server, and it covers only the United States. A drive outside the US, with a stop more than 30 miles from an interstate, or with no interstate route between its two ends, and every train and cruise leg, keep a gently curved line between the two places instead, as the boat does between ports. Each stop's label shows its place and the days you were there, as the dates read at the place; the labels appear in the top-left corner of the map rather than beside the stop, fading in as the path reaches the stop and out a moment later, the last stop's included, so the finished map shows no label (the list below it has them all); pausing or scrubbing keeps the current stop's label on screen. **Pause**, **Replay** and the slider (which the arrow keys move) let you stop, replay or jump to any point, and the stops are listed in order below the map. The replay takes two seconds a stop (at least six seconds, and at most a minute), so a long trip plays slowly enough to read each label; the speed button beside Pause steps through 1×, 2× and 4× for that page only and is not remembered. A faster speed shortens the labels too, since each one stays for the same share of its stop. With reduced motion turned on in your system nothing plays or zooms: the map shows the whole path with every stop labelled and no plane, car or boat icons (they only appear while travelling).

A stop's coordinates come from Waypoint's own lists: a flight uses its airports, and a stay, car, train or a cruise's embarkation uses the point stored with the booking when it was saved ([Where a booking is on the map](#where-a-booking-is-on-the-map)), so a hotel in a town with no airport appears, and two hotels in places more than 30 km apart are two stops. A booking with no stored point falls back to the city it names when an airport in the list is in a city of that name, and a cruise's ports of call always use that city lookup. A booking with no city, or one the lists don't know, is left out of the route rather than guessed; a trip with fewer than two located stops shows no button. The map is drawn from outlines that ship with the app, so no map service is contacted.

With reduced motion turned on in your system, nothing animates: the map shows the whole path with every stop labelled. Either way, the stops are listed in order under the map as text.

### Starting and ending a road trip at home

A road trip (a trip with stays, rental cars or visits but no flight, train or cruise, cancelled bookings not counted) has no booking for getting there and back, so its replay would begin at the first stay and end at the last. Set a **Home** under **Settings → Travel → Home** and the replay starts there, visits the stops in order and returns there as the last stop, which shows as “Back in” your home city like any place a trip returns to. Home is one place for the whole household, empty until someone sets it, with a **Clear** button to empty it again (`GET`, `POST` and `DELETE /api/home`).

- **City level only.** Home is a city, a state or region, an optional ZIP or postal code and a country (the one most of your flights are in, until you change it). There is no street address to enter, and none is kept. The replay only needs a point, and Waypoint puts it at the ZIP or the city from the lists that ship with it ([Places](/Waypoint/reference/data-sources/#places)), never a street, and asks no outside service. If it can't place the city, the box says “Waypoint can’t place this on the map.” and the replay stays as it was.
- **What the map shows.** The first and last stops are labelled with the home city's name and drawn as a house, never the word “Home” or an address. The legs to and from Home use the same roads and labels as the other legs, and a straight line when no road is drawn for one. A trip whose first or last stop is already in your home city doesn't get a second stop there. A trip with a flight, a train or a cruise is left as it is.
- **Where it is kept.** Home is a household setting stored in the database with the other settings, so it is in [backups](/Waypoint/start/docker/#moving-your-data-from-another-machine) and gone when the data is. It reaches the browser only in a trip's map data, for people who can see that trip, and signed-in members can read it in Settings. It is not used for Stats, distances, the calendar feed, reminders or any booking, it is never logged, it is not sent to the AI fallback, and an [assistant](/Waypoint/start/mcp/) cannot reach it.

## In a backup

Trips, segments, their costs and their travellers are part of a [backup](/Waypoint/start/docker/#moving-your-data-from-another-machine) and come back exactly as they were, times and zones included. The airport list and the lists used to place bookings on the map aren’t: every Waypoint has them already.

## Open in app, or the vendor's website

A booking whose email carried no manage link still gets a button on a phone for the vendor it is with: **Open in app** where the vendor's app claims a neutral page of its site (its trips list, or its home page), and **Open website** where it doesn't, so the button never promises an app it can't open. A manage link from the email always wins, and a computer shows no button. The links are ordinary `https` web addresses the phone hands to the installed app (universal links), so nothing is sent about whether the app is installed, no custom `app://` address is used and the link carries nothing about the booking.

- Opens the app: Delta, United, American, Marriott, Hyatt, Hertz, Enterprise, Booking.com, Expedia and Airbnb.
- Opens the website: every other vendor Waypoint knows, because its app claims only account, check-in or parameter-driven pages, or none could be read. Southwest, JetBlue, Alaska, Hilton, IHG, Avis, Budget, Amtrak, Carnival and Royal Caribbean were read and found to claim nothing suitable.

Your phone decides in the end. A universal link doesn't open the app when it's typed into Safari's address bar or tapped on a page of the same site, and the phone remembers a choice of "Open in Safari" for a site. On Android a vendor's app can open the browser instead where it doesn't list that site. The table is `waypoint/domain/app_links.py`, with the date each vendor was checked and the claimed page its link comes from; `python3 tools/app_link_check.py delta.com united.com` fetches those vendors' public `apple-app-site-association` files and prints the production apps and the pages they claim, so the table can be checked again.

If your session expires while a form has unsaved changes, **Sign in** opens in a new tab so the form stays on this page; sign in there, come back and save.
