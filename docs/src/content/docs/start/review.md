**Review** is where Waypoint asks for help, in two lists. A number on its tab says how much is waiting.

## Couldn’t read

When a message looks like a booking (it’s from an airline, hotel, rental company, railway or booking site, and it has a confirmation word in it) but Waypoint can’t get a booking out of it, the message lands here instead of being guessed at or dropped. Each item shows who it came from, the day it was sent and why (not its subject: that is email content, which Waypoint doesn’t keep):

- **No booking details found**: the email carries no machine-readable booking, and Waypoint doesn’t have a parser for that sender yet.
- **Some details missing**: it has booking markup, but not enough to make a segment (no arrival time, an airport Waypoint doesn’t know, a hotel whose time zone Waypoint can’t work out, or times marked as UTC that could be the local clock: see [Scanning](/Waypoint/start/gmail/#scanning)).
- **Couldn’t be opened**: the message itself was damaged.

For each one you can:

- **View email**, to read the message here without adding it. It opens under the item with the same view as **Add by hand**, and choosing **View email** again closes it.
- **Ask AI** (when the optional [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/) are on): asks the model about this one message now, and offers what it read as a suggestion to check. A suggestion the scan asked for by itself (see [Reading unreadable mail automatically](/Waypoint/start/ai/#reading-unreadable-mail-automatically)) arrives already filled in, with a **read automatically** label beside **AI suggestion**. When two or more items have no suggestion yet, **Ask AI about all** above the list asks about each of them in turn; it stops at the first failure and says how many it got through. Any member who can see an item can ask, including an item from a [mailbox shared with the household](#sharing-a-mailboxs-items-with-the-household).
- **Add by hand**: a form for the booking, with the sender’s name filled in as the provider and the message beside it, so you can read the details and type them in one place. The form and message open in a sheet over the dimmed page, on every screen size: it slides up from the bottom on a phone and is a side panel about 40 rem wide on a larger screen, so the list underneath keeps its place. The message is above the form, so you can read the booking while you fill it in; **Hide the email** collapses it to its subject when you need the room, and **Show the email** brings it back. **Cancel**, the close button, tapping outside the sheet, Escape and (on a phone) swiping it down close it; if you changed anything it first asks **Discard this booking?**, and **Keep editing** returns to the form. Nothing closes it while it is saving. Opening another item’s form replaces this one. The message’s **Larger view** opens above the sheet. A hotel has one time zone, for check-in and check-out alike: type it, or leave it empty and give the hotel’s address, and Waypoint works it out from that (it asks again if the address doesn’t say). A message with an HTML part shows as it was formatted (headings, lists, tables, bold and links); one without shows its text. The message is the copy Waypoint kept, so it opens without Gmail, for anyone who sees the item (an item from before messages were kept is fetched from its owner’s Gmail the first time, and kept from then on). The formatted view is rebuilt on the server from a short list of safe tags, so nothing the sender wrote runs: scripts, styles and forms are dropped, and **pictures are never loaded** (you see their description instead), so opening the message doesn’t tell the sender you did. Links open in a new tab without the page they came from, and only `https` and `mailto` ones are kept; a link goes to the sender’s own address, which may identify you, so choosing one is visiting their site. Long messages are cut at 30,000 characters. A booking added from Review can be a flight, hotel stay, car rental, train, cruise or parking; a cruise added there has no ports of call until you add them on the booking. Saving it adds the booking to your trips, takes the item off the list and keeps the message with the booking (see below).
- **Add another leg or booking**: one email often holds more than one thing, such as a round trip, a connecting itinerary, or a flight with a hotel and a car. Under the form, **Add another leg or booking** adds a second form below the first, with the same kind, provider and confirmation code filled in, and, for a flight, the previous flight’s destination as its origin; change any of it, including what it is (changing the kind empties the places, time zones and details that belonged to the old kind). Each leg is titled “Flight 2 of 3: DEN → BOS” (“Booking 2 of 3” when they are not all flights) and has its own **Dismiss this flight** or **Dismiss this booking** button; the last one left can’t be dismissed, close the sheet for that. The same button is there when you check a suggestion, to add one more to what the AI read. **Add 3 flights** (or **Add 3 bookings**) checks every leg before adding any, asks about each against your trips as above, then adds them in order and takes the item off the list once, after the last. If one can’t be added, the ones already added stay added and are gone from the form, the one that failed stays with what you typed, and the item stays in the list until the rest are added or you close the sheet. The discard question counts an added leg as a change.
- Items can include messages Gmail files under Purchases or Reservations that say “confirmation” or “booking” but that Waypoint couldn’t read, such as a restaurant or an appointment. Dismiss them, or use the next action.
- **Not travel: ignore this sender**: for shopping and other mail that was never travel. After asking, with the number of items that will go, later scans skip that sender’s mail for this mailbox, and its other items leave the list, their kept messages deleted with them. Items that hold bookings to settle stay, and a booking already made from one of their emails is never touched. The confirmation says where to undo it, and warns when the sender is an airline, hotel group or other travel company Waypoint knows, since ignoring it means its bookings won’t be added (it is still allowed). Right after you confirm, the message at the bottom of the screen has an **Undo** button for a few seconds; after that, undo it from the mailbox’s **Ignored senders** list in [Settings](/Waypoint/start/gmail/#ignored-senders).
- **Review at a glance** (Settings → Mail and AI → Gmail, under each mailbox) counts what is waiting by reason and by sender and links each sender here, showing only that sender’s mail; see [Review at a glance](/Waypoint/start/gmail/#review-at-a-glance).
- **Dismiss**: it isn’t a booking, so take it off. Waypoint remembers it has read the message, so it doesn’t come back.

- **Several emails about one reservation show as one row.** A reservation sends a confirmation, a receipt, a check-in notice and reminders, and when none of them can be read they would each wait here. When waiting items are in the same mailbox, come from the same sender domain and each name the same confirmation code (a code written after a label such as “Confirmation code” or “Booking reference”; a message that names two codes, or none, is never grouped), Waypoint shows them as one row, “3 emails about the same reservation”, with the newest first and the others listed under it. **Dismiss all** takes every one of them off and deletes their stored copies, as dismissing one does. **Add by hand** or **Check suggestion** works on the newest email; once the booking is added, that email stays with the booking and the others are removed. The number on the Review tab counts rows, not emails.
- **A booking that is already in your trips is added to, not repeated.** When you choose **Add to my trips** (or **Add 2 flights**) and what you typed is the same reservation as one you can see (the same kind and confirmation code, starting within three days of it or overlapping its dates, the same flight number and airports for a flight, and not a cancelled one), Waypoint stops and says so, even when the times, the way a place or company is written, or the company itself differ: “This looks like a booking you already have”, with the booking and its trip. **Add the email to that booking** keeps the message with the existing booking and makes no second one: nothing already on it changes, only what it lacked is filled in. **Add as a separate booking** adds it as you typed it, and **Go back** returns to the form. With several suggestions, the ones that match are attached and the rest are added. A booking you can’t see is never offered.
- **Follow-up mail for a booking you already have never gets here.** A later email that can’t be made into a booking, but carries the confirmation code of exactly one booking you can see, from the same company (the sender’s domain is the one the booking’s email came from, or contains the booking’s provider name), is attached to that booking quietly: it creates no item and nothing of it is kept. Waypoint never does this from dates or names alone, never for an email whose subject, or whose text, says a booking has been cancelled, changed or rescheduled (a cancellation policy alone doesn’t count) (those still wait here for a person), never when the code fits bookings in two trips, two different codes fit, or the code fits only a booking you can’t see, and it deletes nothing in Gmail. When the code fits several bookings you can see in one trip (the legs of one flight reservation, say), it is attached to the reservation and recorded with its first booking, and a total it states isn’t added to any of them, since it can’t tell which leg it belongs to. A follow-up that mentions a seat, gate, terminal or address the booking doesn’t have isn’t applied; it’s counted in the scan’s figures in Settings.

Items are private to the member whose mailbox they came from: nobody else sees them, or even that they exist, until that member chooses to share the mailbox. Each item shows its message’s **subject**, and Waypoint keeps the message itself, encrypted, for as long as it’s needed: until the item is dismissed or added, and for as long as a booking made from it exists. That includes the emails a scan turned into bookings: a booking made from an email has a **View email** button on its card in the trip, for anyone who can see the booking, so the original is there when you need to check it. The message is shown as it was sent, with its layout and pictures, inline in the page, in an isolated container that can run nothing and reaches nothing outside itself (see [Email scanning](/Waypoint/privacy/email-scanning/#what-the-sender-can-learn)), so it scrolls, selects text and follows links like the rest of the app; its styles can’t change Waypoint’s, and Waypoint’s can’t change its (an email is shown on its own light page background, in a bordered box, the way a mail client shows it, so the app’s dark theme never changes its colours or fonts); an email kept before that was added shows its text and simple formatting, with a line saying the original can’t be shown. The preview **fits the screen width** by default: an email laid out for a fixed width wider than the space it has (most are about 600 to 700 pixels wide) is shown scaled down so you can see all of it without scrolling sideways, and one that is narrower or flexible is shown at normal size. The zoom buttons below the preview zoom in and out in steps of 25% (from the fit size up to 300%, with the current zoom shown) and **Fit** goes back to the default; zoomed past the width of the box, the preview scrolls inside itself. Because the email is part of the page, pinching zooms the page and the email together, the way it does anywhere else in Waypoint. **Larger view** opens the email in a full-screen window with the same controls, starting at fit; Escape or **Close** returns you to the card. Zoom isn’t remembered: each preview opens at fit. An email with no formatting is shown as plain text, which wraps its own text, and an email kept before the original could be shown has nothing to zoom. Waypoint measures the layout from the email itself in your browser, so nothing about its width is stored or sent. Links in an email (only `https` and `mailto` ones are kept) open in a new tab and never take you away from Waypoint. Dismissing an item deletes its message, pictures included (unless a booking was made from it), as does removing the last booking made from it or disconnecting the mailbox.

### Sharing a mailbox’s items with the household

In **Settings → Mail and AI → Gmail**, each mailbox has a box, **Show this mailbox’s unread mail to the household**, off until its owner ticks it (only the owner can, and unticking it hides the items again at once). While it’s on, every member sees that mailbox’s items in Review, marked with whose mailbox they’re in, and the number on the Review tab counts them. They see the same things the owner does (its subject, who it came from, the day, why it couldn’t be read, and what the optional AI read from it) and can **View email**, **Add by hand** (with the message beside the form, as the owner does) or **Dismiss** an item, so anyone can clear the queue. **Ask AI** is open to every member who sees the item (see [AI suggestions](/Waypoint/start/ai/#who-can-ask)); **Not travel: ignore this sender** stays with the mailbox’s owner. A mailbox, and what it shares, ends with its owner’s access.

After each scan, Waypoint’s log says how many messages it read and what stopped the others, as counts of fixed phrases (“12 × no structured booking data”, “3 × unknown airport”, “2 × arrival time”), never which messages or anything they said. Most senders put no machine-readable booking in their emails; those are the ones to add by hand, or to ignore.

## Could be one of your bookings

When a booking email (one with a confirmation code) fits more than one booking you added by hand without a code (a stay with the same check-in and check-out days, time zone and name, or a flight with the same number, day and airports), Waypoint can’t tell which it is, so it doesn’t add the email and doesn’t guess. An item here shows the email’s booking and each booking it could be, with its trip.

- **It’s this one** fills in that booking with the email’s confirmation code, provider and link (a field you edited by hand is left as it is), and the email is kept with it, as for any booking made from an email.
- **It’s a new booking** adds the email as a booking of its own.
- **Dismiss** takes that booking off and adds nothing. If the same email held another booking, that one stays until you settle it too. When the email was also one Waypoint couldn’t fully read, it stays in the list above for that.

The bookings it could be are shown only to people who can see them: someone else in the household who sees the item (a [shared mailbox](#sharing-a-mailboxs-items-with-the-household)) sees it without the bookings that aren’t theirs, and can still add it as a new booking. These items don’t go to the AI. The message itself stays on the server like any other Review item’s.

## Who is this?

A booking prints each traveller’s name its own way (`DOE/JANE MS`). Waypoint matches a name to a person by the loyalty number on the booking first, then by the person’s display name, legal name or aliases on [People](/Waypoint/start/people/); it never picks between two people with the same name. A name nobody matches stays as printed, and the booking is then visible only to whoever’s mailbox it came from.

A hotel, rental car or parking booking added from an item, like one added by hand, is for everyone on the trip it joins, so its guest name is not kept.

Each such name is listed here with the segment it’s on. Choose the person it is, or add a guest. Every traveller with that printed name on your trips becomes that person, and the printed name is kept as one of their aliases, so the next booking with it matches by itself.

## Letting an AI assistant help

An assistant you connected over [MCP](/Waypoint/start/mcp/#the-review-queue) can list these items, compare each with your trips (with a confidence of exact, likely or possible, and the fields that agreed), and, if you allowed changes, match, add or dismiss them for you. It sees only what Waypoint read from each email, not the email, unless you switched on **Read email text** for that connection in Settings.

## When a scan has a problem

A scan that stops halfway (Google couldn’t be reached, or refused a request) keeps everything it had already read and says what failed under the mailbox in Settings; the next scan carries on where it stopped. A scan that can’t start at all, because the mailbox needs reconnecting, is shown as **Reconnect**, not as a failed scan.
