# Photos on any trip, from Immich

Status: accepted design, being built. On main: the photo tables (#486, migration 0038), the matcher (#488), the trip photos reply and Needs a look (#491) and the trip summary (#493). Not built yet: per-trip consent, exclusion and sharing scope (this revision, from #504; migration 0039), the sources API (#489), the sync job (#490), the proxy (#492) and the web app (#496, #497, #498, #499). The parts already on main change as "Impact on the issues" at the end lists.

## What it does

Every trip, past or future, can get its photos. Waypoint reads each family member's Immich library (Darkroom builds one library per person on Unraid; Darkroom never talks to Waypoint), matches photos to trips by capture time and then by distance to the trip's bookings, and shows them on the trip page: a strip and gallery, pins on the route map, and a line of trip stats. Immich stays the source of truth: Waypoint keeps a reference per photo (asset id, time, location, match) and streams thumbnails and originals from Immich through its own authenticated routes, so Immich is never exposed to the browser.

A person's photos are used on a trip only when that person has shared them on that trip (a **grant**), and each grant says who sees them: **Only me** (the default) or **Trip** (the other people on the trip). A person can take single photos off a trip (**Remove from this trip**, stored as an **exclusion**) and put them back, and can take back what they shared at any time: one photo, a whole trip (**Stop sharing on this trip**), or by switching a trip back to Only me. **Share on all my trips** is a standing setting that shares every trip, including ones joined later, without asking. Every removal takes effect at once, for every viewer and every path ("Who sees a photo" below).

Nothing is special-cased for one trip. The motivating case (a two-year road trip of stays and rentals) is just a long trip with many `hotel` and `car` segments; a weekend flight works the same way.

Non-goals for the first version: editing, albums, faces, uploading, sharing links, photos in the offline copy, photos through `/mcp`, and map tiles (Waypoint's maps stay vector, drawn from bundled data).

## Terms

- **The photo's person**: the person in People whose library the photo came from (`photo_sources.person_id`).
- **Acting for a person**: a signed-in member acts for the person their sign-in is linked to, and once a person has a sign-in only they act for themselves. A person with a sign-in connects their own libraries only: `POST /api/photo-sources` for them is accepted only from their own sign-in, so another member can never attach a library to them and have their grants share it. A person with no sign-in of their own (a child) has one **steward**, recorded on the person's sharing row (`photo_sharing_prefs.steward_sub`) when their first library is connected, independently of any source, so deleting the child's last library does not make way for another member to inherit their sharing. All of such a person's sources have the steward as `owner_sub`: connecting a library for a person without a sign-in whose library another member already connected is refused with a fixed sentence ("This person's library is managed by another member; ask them to connect it"), so connecting a library never gives a member authority over photos from a library someone else connected. The steward's authority ends with their access, and only then can another member become steward: when they can no longer sign in their sources end (`photos.end_lapsed`), and `photogrants.end_lapsed` deletes the grants they are accountable for (with their derived rows), turns the standing setting off and clears `steward_sub`, so a new steward starts with nothing shared. When a person without a sign-in gets one (their People entry is linked to a sign-in), `photos.claim(conn, person, sub)` hands everything over in that same transaction: their sources' `owner_sub`, their grants' `granted_by` and their preferences' `set_by` become the new sign-in and `steward_sub` is cleared, and, because claiming a guest merges the guest's person into the member's and deletes the guest, the same transaction moves every photo table's `person_id` from the guest to the member before that delete, so nothing cascades away: sources move (a guest source at an address the member already has is merged into the member's only when both name the same Immich user, `immich_user_id`: its `photos`, `photo_trip_syncs` and hand placements move to the member's source and it is dropped; a source for a different Immich user on the same server is kept as it is, so the source address is unique per person and Immich user, not per address alone), `photos` and `photo_trip_syncs` follow their source, grants move and where both have one for a trip the more restrictive scope is kept (a revoked row beats an active one, Only me beats Trip) with the member's `ask_again` and `granted_by` winning, exclusions are unioned (a conflict keeps one row), and the preferences row is the member's own if they have one (the guest's is dropped) and otherwise the guest's, so from then on only the person can change, pause, re-key or remove their libraries and their sharing, and the former steward keeps nothing (no counts, no source controls, no sharing). The same transaction runs `photogrants.reconcile` for every trip the person is on, because with their own sign-in they may see trips their steward couldn't, and with **Share on all my trips** on those are shared now; the resulting syncs are queued after commit. Photos are off in local mode (no sign-in): there are no photo sources, grants or sharing there, the photo routes answer 404, the sync and the sweep do nothing, and the web app shows no Photos section, prompt, card, pin or summary figure (decision 22). Only someone acting for a person can grant, change the scope of, stop sharing, remove, move or put back that person's photos and set their sharing preferences, and they see that person's photos as the person does, on the trips they can see themselves: acting for someone never widens trip visibility, so a steward cannot see, share or stop sharing a child's photos on a trip the steward can't see.
- **Grant**: one row of `photo_grants`, for one (person, trip), active while `revoked` is null.
- **Scope**: `only_me` (default) or `trip`.
- **Exclusion**: one row of `photo_exclusions`, for one (person, Immich asset, trip): what **Remove from this trip** writes and **Put back** deletes.
- **The trip's owner**: whoever booked or created a trip. They get no special access to photos: the predicate below has no case for them, and neither does the household's admin, and they cannot remove someone else's photo. Where this file says "the owner" of a photo decision (`decided_by = owner`, "the owner picks"), it means whoever acts for the photo's person, never the trip's owner.

Promises touched (AGENTS.md, "Waypoint's promises"):

- **You see the trips you're on.** Photo routes answer 404 for a trip the viewer can't see, exactly like `GET /api/trips/{id}/places`. For photos, seeing the trip is necessary but not enough: another person's photo also needs that person's active grant with scope Trip, the viewer to be one of the trip's people (not just its booker or a confirmation recipient), and no exclusion. Unlike bookings and costs, a trip's photos are not the same for everyone who can see it.
- **Times are where they happen.** A photo's time is shown as the wall-clock time where it was taken, never the viewer's zone.
- **Private data in logs.** Sync and proxy log counts and fixed error texts, never an Immich URL with a key, an asset id with a trip name, a place, or who granted what on which trip.

A new line for the promises list, added to AGENTS.md by the pull request that ships the proxy (#492), with the checks it names: "**Photos are shared by their person.** Photos stay in Immich. Waypoint keeps a photo's asset id, when and where it was taken and which trip it matched, nothing else, and only for trips its person shared their photos on. A photo reaches someone else only while its person's grant for that trip is active with the Trip scope, the viewer is one of the trip's people, and it is not excluded; everything else (the pixels through `GET /api/photos/{id}/…`, the trip photos reply, pins, clusters, the replay, the summary and its counts, Needs a look) is built from the photos that one predicate lets the viewer see, so nothing says that photos they can't see exist. Taking a photo back (removing it from the trip, stopping sharing, switching to Only me) takes effect on the next request, and no reply lets a browser show a photo again without asking. The Immich address and key never reach the browser. Checked: no `Photo` query outside `photos.visible` (Semgrep's `waypoint-photo-access`), `tests/test_photo_access.py` (every path, for the person, a trip member under each scope, a stranger, the trip's owner and an assistant, before and after each removal), `tests/test_every_route.py`, and `no_leaks` with canary asset ids and places."

## Data model

Migration `0038_photos` (on main, #486) added `photo_sources`, `photos` and `photo_trip_syncs`. Migration `0039_photo_grants` (after 0038, with its `test_0039_…` in `tests/test_migrations.py`) adds `photo_grants`, `photo_exclusions` and `photo_sharing_prefs` and drops `photos.hidden` (below). Tables are declared in `waypoint/storage/schema.py` with `info={'doc': …}` and mapped in `waypoint/storage/models.py`. All six are in a backup (none joins `backup.SKIP`): the key travels encrypted as mailbox tokens do, the photo rows hold the person's decisions (moved to another trip), and the grants, exclusions and sharing defaults hold what each person chose to share, which a re-sync must not undo.

**`photos`**: one Immich asset Waypoint has looked at inside a granted trip window. Kept small on purpose: a reference and the match, no pixels, no file names, no camera data.

| Column | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | int pk | Waypoint's own id, the one in URLs (never the Immich id in the browser's address bar, so an asset id is not guessable from outside). |

| `segment_id` | fk `segments.id`, SET NULL | The nearest segment (by distance, else by time). |
| `match` | text, not null | `place` (GPS within NEAR of a segment of its trip), `time` (one trip's window, no GPS or no close segment), `far` (in a window but more than FAR from every point of that trip; matched, flagged), `ambiguous` (two granted windows, distance couldn't settle it; not shown on a trip until the person picks), `home` (within HOME of Home; ignored), `none` (no window). |

Indexes: (`trip_id`), (`segment_id`, so deleting a booking finds its photos without scanning the table), (`source_id`, `taken_utc`), unique (`source_id`, `asset_id`).

`hidden` (added by 0038 for decision 3) is dropped by 0039: hiding a photo from its trip and removing it from its trip are the same thing, and the exclusion is the one mechanism (decision 3 and decision 15). 0039 first copies each `hidden = true` row that has a `trip_id` into `photo_exclusions` (its source's person, its asset id, its trip) as one `INSERT … SELECT DISTINCT`, so the same asset hidden through two sources of one person on one trip becomes one exclusion, not a key collision (`test_0039_…` seeds that duplicate), then deletes every `photos` and `photo_trip_syncs` row, because no grant exists when 0039 runs and rows without a grant are not kept; the sync fetches the granted windows afresh. Nothing is live (the sync, #490, is not built), so this loses no one's photos, and no tripless hidden row (a Leave off made on an ambiguous photo, which only the sync creates) can exist yet; the upgrade deletes any it finds and `test_0039_…` seeds none. In the other direction the downgrade deletes every tripless `ambiguous` row of a person with a Leave off exclusion (0038 cannot hide a row from its person) and `test_0039_…` seeds one. Its downgrade must not widen anything, because 0038's model shows every row on a trip to everyone who can see it: before dropping the three tables it deletes every `photos` row whose trip has no active grant for its person, deletes every row with an exclusion for its asset on the row's own trip (person, asset id and the row's `trip_id`; 0038 cannot represent a photo hidden even from its person, since `hidden` would show it to them again), while a row on trip B whose asset was removed only from an overlapping trip A is kept, as 0038 can show it, and sets `hidden = true` on every remaining row under an `only_me` grant, since 0038 shows a hidden photo to its person alone, and on every row under a `trip` grant whose trip has a viewer who is not one of its people (a booker or confirmation recipient not on it), since 0038 would show it to them and 0039 does not; then it drops the tables, so after a downgrade nobody sees a photo they could not see before it. `test_0039_…` holds the round trip: rows under each scope, a Trip row on a trip with a booker who is not one of its people (hidden after the downgrade), a removed row (gone after the downgrade), a row on trip B whose asset was removed only from overlapping trip A (kept), and an ungranted one, upgraded, downgraded, and checked for who would see what.

Primary key (`person_id`, `trip_id`), which also serves "this person's grants"; index (`trip_id`) for a trip's grants (every trip photos read joins on it, and a trip's deletion cascades through it). A grant ends with its accountable member's access, as AGENTS.md asks of every new kind of grant: `photogrants.end_lapsed`, in `jobs.sweep_lapsed` next to `photos.end_lapsed`, asks `oidc.access_lapsed` about each grant's `granted_by`, revoked rows included (a declined reminder must not pass to a replacement steward), and deletes each one whose member can no longer sign in, re-placing the derived rows of the active ones as **Stop sharing** does; with no row left, the person is asked again (or a new steward shares). A grant the system ends (a lapse, or its steward no longer seeing the trip, below) is deleted, never kept revoked, so `revoked` only ever records a person's own Not now or Stop sharing. A grant written by the standing setting has the setting's `set_by` as its `granted_by`.

## Immich, as verified

Checked against the Immich API docs (v1.140 reference) on 2026-11-01; the spike confirms them on the family server, which may run a different version.

- **API keys are per user**, made under Account Settings, sent as the `x-api-key` header, and can be scoped to permissions (from 1.138; older servers need `all`). Waypoint asks for `asset.read` and `asset.view` (thumbnails and previews), `user.read` (the Test step calls `GET /api/users/me`, which Immich protects with it), `map.read` (`GET /api/map/markers` needs it), and `asset.download`, because originals are served through Waypoint (decision 6).
- **`POST /api/search/metadata`** (searchAssets) takes `takenAfter` and `takenBefore` (date-time), `updatedAfter`, `page` (≥ 1), `size` (1–1000), `withExif`, `order` (`asc`/`desc`), `type` (`IMAGE`, `VIDEO`), `visibility` (`timeline`, `archive`, `hidden`, `locked`), `libraryId`. Reply: `assets.items`, `assets.nextPage`, `assets.total`, `assets.count`. Each asset: `id`, `type`, `localDateTime`, `fileCreatedAt` (UTC), `updatedAt`, `exifInfo.dateTimeOriginal`, `exifInfo.timeZone`, `exifInfo.latitude`, `exifInfo.longitude`, `exifInfo.city`, `exifInfo.state`, `exifInfo.country`, `ownerId`. Waypoint keeps only assets whose `ownerId` is the key's own Immich user (`photo_sources.immich_user_id`); assets a partner shares with that user are dropped, so one person's library never brings in another person's photos.
- **`GET /api/map/markers`** takes `fileCreatedAfter`, `fileCreatedBefore`, `isArchived`, `isFavorite`, `withPartners`, `withSharedAlbums` and returns `{id, lat, lon, city, state, country}` per located asset. It has no time per marker, so the design uses metadata search as the one source and keeps markers as an optional cheap pre-pass the spike may recommend.
- **`GET /api/assets/{id}/thumbnail?size=thumbnail|preview|fullsize`** (viewAsset, `asset.view`) and **`GET /api/assets/{id}/original`** (`asset.download`).
- **`GET /api/server/ping`** / `GET /api/users/me` (`user.read`) to test a connection and learn the key's user: the name is shown once in Settings as "connected as …"; the user id is stored in `photo_sources.immich_user_id` to bind the source to that account.

## Consent: grants, scope and removal

The screens are the signed-off designs (i)–(m) in `docs/design/photos/` (#485, #504); the names in **bold** below are their button and section names.

**Asking.** The trip page calls `GET /api/trips/{id}/photo-sharing`, which answers only about the viewer's own person (never anyone else's): `shared` with its scope, `declined` (a revoked row), `ask` or `none`, and for `shared` and `declined` the count of their photos on the trip and of their removed ones. `ask` needs either a grant with `ask_again` set (after the trip grew; asked whatever the standing setting says), or no grant row and their **Share on all my trips** off; and in both cases the person in the trip's people (below), and at least one asset in the trip's window across the person's sources: `photos.unshared_count(conn, person, trip)` searches each of the person's sources that is not `off` live with `search/metadata` (`takenAfter`/`takenBefore` of the window, `visibility: "timeline"`, `withExif: true`, `size: 1000`), keeps only the assets whose `ownerId` is that source's `immich_user_id` (Immich also returns partner-timeline assets, which the sync drops too), and counts those the sync would put on this trip if it were shared: the same pure matcher, with Home suppression, the person's granted trips plus this one as candidates, and their exclusions for every candidate trip (an asset removed from an overlapping trip that is already granted is not a candidate for it, as in the real sync), counting `place`, `time` and `far`; the photos are de-duplicated by (person, asset id) across sources, so one asset reached through two sources counts once, and then counted; the sum is the count the prompt shows ("You have 77 photos from this trip"), to the viewer alone. A window of many pages is counted page by page under the request's time budget; `assets.total` is not used, because it counts partner assets as well. If any of the person's sources that is not `off` can't be queried (status `refused` or `unreachable`, or the request fails or runs out of time), the whole check fails: the answer is `none`, Settings shows "Couldn't check your libraries; try again later" for that trip, and the trip asks again next time. A partial total is never shown, so no one grants on a count that left a library out. A person whose sources are all `off`, or who has none, gets `none`. Nothing from these requests is stored in the database. The prompt (`PhotoGrantPrompt.svelte`, design i) is a card at the top of the trip's Photos section, above what others have shared, saying "Only you see this. Nothing from your library is on the trip until you share it.", with the radio group **Who can see them**: **Only me** (preselected from `photo_sharing_prefs.default_scope`, Only me unless changed) or **Everyone on this trip**, and three buttons:

- **Share on this trip**: a grant with the chosen scope; a toast says "Shared with you only. Change it any time under Your photos on this trip." (or the Everyone wording).
- **Share on all my trips**: a grant with the chosen scope on this trip and on every trip the person is on now that has no row yet (including trips declined with Not now or stopped earlier, after the confirmation below), and it turns on the standing **Share on all my trips** setting with that scope as the default (`photo_sharing_prefs.share_all` and `default_scope`), so trips they join later are shared the same way without asking (decision 14).
- **Not now**: a revoked row, when there was no grant. On the re-ask after a trip grew (`ask_again` on an active Only me grant), Not now only clears `ask_again`: the grant stays active and Only me, nothing is deleted, and nothing is shown to anyone else. The card folds into a one-line reminder only the person sees ("Your 77 photos from this trip aren't on it. Only you see this.") whose button **Share your photos on this trip** opens the prompt again; Settings lists the trip as **Not shared** with **Share…**.

A person with no sign-in is never prompted; their steward shares for them in Settings (design m).

**Granting** writes or clears the row and queues a sync of that person's sources for that trip, as **Sync now** does, so photos appear within a run rather than at the next schedule. Every sync a request asks for (a grant, Put back, turning on **Share on all my trips**) is queued with `photosync.after_commit` and its thread starts only once the request's transaction has committed (`routes.dispatch` commits after the handler returns), so the run always sees the grant or the deleted exclusion; a request that fails and rolls back starts nothing. A grant written by `photogrants.joined` inside another change (a booking moved) is queued the same way.

**Only people on the trip.** Seeing a trip is not enough to share on it: a viewer can see a trip as its booker or as a confirmation recipient without being one of its people. The prompt answers `ask`, and a grant (from the prompt, `PUT /api/trips/{id}/photo-sharing` or the standing setting) is accepted, only when the person it is for is in the trip's people (`trippeople.of`); otherwise the reply is `none` and a grant is refused with the same 404 as a trip the viewer can't see, so nothing is ever granted on a trip where the person's photos could never be matched.

**The standing setting.** While `share_all` is on, a trip the person joins (`trippeople` gains them) gets a grant at `default_scope` in the same transaction, and a sync starts, without asking; with it off, they start with no grant and are asked when they first open the trip (decision 14). Turning it on is a widening, so it asks once ("Share your photos on all your trips? This includes N trips you haven't shared or stopped sharing.", **Cancel** / **Share on all**), then grants every current trip of the person that is eligible (they are on it, the setter can see it) at `default_scope`, clearing revoked rows too, so while it is on every such trip with their photos is shared, as the Settings copy says. A trip whose sharing they stop while the setting is on stays stopped: the setting writes only trips with no row and trips joined later. Turning it off grants and revokes nothing. Changing the default scope changes no trip already shared. It only ever grants trips that whoever set it (`photo_sharing_prefs.set_by`) can see (Terms), so a steward's setting never shares a child's photos on a trip the steward can't see. Because the setter can come to see a trip in other ways than the person joining it (the steward books it, or a confirmation for it arrives in their mailbox), `photogrants.reconcile(conn, trip)` runs in the transaction of every change that can change who sees a trip or who is on it (a booking added, moved or removed, a booker changed, a confirmation attached, a merge), and works both ways, in that transaction: it grants each person on the trip whose setting is on and whose setter can now see it, when they have no row there or only a revoked row older than `share_all_since` (a decline from before the setting, which turning it on would have re-shared); and it deletes each active grant on the trip whose `granted_by` can no longer see it (a steward who lost sight of the trip can't reach its controls, so the sharing they made there ends, its rows re-placed). `sync_all` runs it for every trip too, as a backstop.

**Your photos on this trip** (design j) is a card at the end of the trip's Photos section, shown only to someone whose library has photos on the trip: the count, a scope chip, the radio group **Who can see your photos on this trip**, **Stop sharing on this trip**, and the disclosure **Removed from this trip (N)**. When the person's photos are shared the Photos section also shows them a one-line row ("Your 171 photos here are shared with everyone on this trip.") with **Manage**.

**Scope.** Switching between Only me and Everyone updates `scope`, `changed` and `granted_by`; no photo row moves or is deleted, because every read applies the predicate below at request time. Narrowing to **Only me** saves at once, without asking, and is a removal that takes effect on the next request; a toast "Now only you see your photos on this trip." offers **Undo**, which is a widening: it sends the trip's window and the `changed` value of the grant it narrowed (and is refused with 409 unless the active grant is still that exact Only me grant, so a stale toast can't undo a newer state), and if the window has changed since (the trip grew or shrank) the server refuses it (409) and the toast gives way to "The trip changed. Review who sees your photos under Your photos on this trip.", so an Undo can never expose photos the person did not confirm. Widening to everyone asks once ("Show your 171 photos to everyone on this trip?", **Cancel** / **Show them**).

**Remove from this trip** (design k), on the person's own photos only, from the viewer or from the gallery's select mode (**Select**, several at once; other people's tiles can't be selected), writes one `photo_exclusions` row per photo (`POST /api/trips/{id}/photo-exclusions`), with an **Undo** toast. In the same transaction the photo is matched again without that trip (Matching): usually no candidate is left and its row stays where it was, shown nowhere, because the person's own card lists it under **Removed from this trip**, as a small tile with **Put back** ("Only you see these. Nobody else on the trip can tell they were here."). Everywhere else it is gone for everyone, its person included: gallery, viewer and its counter, map, replay, summary and counts. **Put back** (or Undo) deletes that exclusion and matches the row again at once with the trip back among its candidates, which returns it to the trip it was removed from, restoring a hand placement exactly when the trip's current window still contains the photo (checked under the grant lock; otherwise the row is matched again like any other) (the removal stores the row's trip as `photo_exclusions.was_trip_id` when `decided_by` was `owner`, and Put back sets the row back to it and to `decided_by = owner`) unless its person has placed it elsewhere by hand since, so the photo is back on the next request, and starts a sync of that trip for the person's sources, as granting does, so a photo whose row the re-list has since deleted comes back too. Nobody else, including the trip's owner, can remove someone else's photo. Removal never touches Immich.

**Stop sharing on this trip** (`DELETE /api/trips/{id}/photo-sharing`, from the card or Settings) asks once, in a `ConfirmDialog`: "Stop sharing your photos on this trip?" ("Your 171 photos leave Four Corners loop right away, for everyone: the gallery, the map, the replay and the trip's counts. Nothing changes in Immich, and you can share them again later.", **Keep sharing** focused first, **Stop sharing**). It sets `revoked`, and in the same transaction re-places the person's `photos` rows with that `trip_id`, and their `ambiguous` rows (no trip) whose candidates included it, among the trips still granted (below; an `ambiguous` row left with one candidate is placed there, with none it is deleted), and deletes their sources' `photo_trip_syncs` rows for the trip. Their tripless `home` rows are re-evaluated too: a `home` row whose capture time falls in no remaining granted window of the person is deleted (a paused or `off` source fetches nothing, so nothing else would), and one still covered by another granted window is kept. Exclusions are kept, so sharing again later brings the photos back with every removal kept.

**Removal**, whichever of the three, is immediate and complete:

- It is committed before the request that asked for it answers, and every path reads through the predicate at request time, so the very next request from anyone (a thumbnail, a preview, an original or a range request of a video already playing, the trip photos reply, the summary) no longer has the photo.
- What each one keeps and deletes. **Remove from this trip** keeps the `photo_exclusions` row (person, asset id, trip) and the photo's row, which on that trip only the person sees, in their removed list; it deletes nothing, since what others saw was never stored. **Stop sharing** keeps the `photo_grants` row with `revoked` set and the person's exclusions for the trip, and deletes the derived match rows (`photos` rows, with their trip, segment, distance, inferred location, state and country) that fall in no other granted trip's window, re-placing the rest on that other trip, and the trip's `photo_trip_syncs` rows. **Switching to Only me** deletes nothing and keeps the grant and the rows, which only the person still sees. In every case no pixels were ever stored, and clusters, pins, counts, the summary's photo places and Needs a look entries are never stored either: each is computed per request from the rows the predicate lets that viewer see, so they change with the rows.
- Browser caches cannot keep showing it: see the cache policy in "Proxy and access rules". A picture already on someone's screen cannot be unseen; everything after that moment honours the removal. Open trip pages fetch the photo list again when they regain focus and every 60 seconds while visible, and drop tiles and pins that left; the viewer checks again on every move and when a preview fails, and a photo taken back gives way to a notice (design l3, the dialog renamed **Photo no longer shared**): "This photo is no longer shared. It was taken off this trip.", with **Next photo**, **Back to the trip** and **Close**, naming nobody.
- A sync running at the moment of a removal cannot undo it. A removal and a sync page write for a (person, trip) both first lock that `photo_grants` row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on Postgres; SQLite's single writer gives the same order), so one waits for the other: the sync, holding the lock, re-reads the grant and the exclusions, writes no row for a revoked grant and never places an asset on a trip it was removed from; a removal that waited applies to what the page wrote (Stop sharing re-places or deletes it, Remove from this trip matches it again without that trip). Under read committed this is what makes the check and the write one step. A page locks the grant rows of every trip it writes to in trip id order, so two locks never deadlock.

## Matching

`photomatch.match(photo, candidates, home) -> Match`, pure. Inputs: the photo (instant, GPS or none, person), the candidate trips with their windows and located points, and the household's Home point (`domain/home.py`, may be none).

**Candidate trips** are the trips the photo's person is on (`trip_people`, `trippeople.of`, which by Waypoint's rules already includes everyone named on any booking) **and** has an active grant for; an asset removed from one of them is not matched to that one (below). A trip the person is on but has not granted is never a candidate, so its window is never searched and none of their photos is matched, shown or counted on it. Being the booker or an email recipient does not make a trip a candidate: a parent who booked a child's trip does not get their own photos matched to it.

**A trip's window** is `[first live segment start − PAD, last live segment end + PAD]` as instants from each segment's own zone (`trips.instant`), falling back to `start_date 00:00` and `end_date 23:59` in the first and last segment's zones when a trip has dates and no times (a visit-only past trip). Cancelled segments don't count. A trip with dates and no live segment at all (a trip created by hand or through the assistant tools before anything is booked) takes the same `start_date 00:00` to `end_date 23:59` in the zone of the Home point when the household has one, else UTC; with no segment there is no point to be near, so its photos can only match by `time`, never `place` or `far`, and its people are the ones the trip lists. A trip with no dates and no live segment has no window and never prompts, grants or syncs.

**Steps**

1. Drop the photo when its instant is in no candidate window: `none`. These rows are never written (the sync only asks Immich inside granted windows), so photos from ordinary days, and from trips not shared, never enter Waypoint.
2. With GPS within the Home radius for Home's precision (table below): `home`, ignored even inside a window (a road trip that starts at home gets its first photos at the first stop, not the driveway). A Home known only to the city has no radius: nothing is suppressed automatically, and a photo near it follows the normal steps (its person can remove or move it).
3. Score each candidate window that contains the instant: the nearest live segment point of that trip (stays, cars, parking, trains and cruises use the point stored with the segment, `segments.latitude/longitude`; a flight uses its two airports; ports via `geocode.locate_port`), preferring segments active at that instant and breaking ties by time distance. Without GPS the score is the time distance to the nearest segment.
4. One candidate: it wins. With GPS: `place` when within NEAR km of a segment point, `far` when farther than FAR km from every point of the trip, else `time`. Without GPS: `time`.
5. Several candidates (an overlapping short trip inside a long one, both granted): with GPS, the trip with the smaller nearest distance wins when it beats the next by at least MARGIN km; otherwise, and always without GPS, the shortest window wins (the more specific trip). When the shortest window isn't unique: `ambiguous`, no trip, the person picks.
6. `segment_id` and `distance_km` are the nearest segment and its distance; a photo without GPS takes `where_was` below.

An exclusion is per trip: it takes that one trip out of the asset's candidates and nothing else. A photo removed from one of two overlapping granted trips is matched among the others as usual (it may land on the other trip, where its person can remove it too), and stays listed in the first trip's removed list. When no candidate is left (removed from its only trip, or **Leave off**, which excludes an `ambiguous` photo from every trip it could belong to), the row keeps its trip (none, for **Leave off**, where it stays `ambiguous` and Needs a look no longer lists it) and is not re-placed, so it is shown nowhere and listed only in its person's removed lists. A removed list is built from the trip's exclusions joined to the person's rows by asset id, whatever trip the row is on now, keeping only rows whose capture time falls in the trip's current window (so exclusions copied to a split trip never list the original's photos). A new row for an asset with exclusions (the first fetch after sharing again, a library reconnected, or an asset a re-list deleted and Immich shows again) is matched the same way, so a removal is never undone by a fetch; and when its only candidates are trips it was removed from, it is still written, as a removed row on the first of those trips (by trip id), rather than dropped as `none`, so the removed list shows it and **Put back** has a row to restore. `tests/test_photosync.py` holds it: remove a photo from its only trip, delete its row as a re-list would, fetch again, and the row is back in that trip's removed list and Put back restores it.

**Itinerary location for a photo without GPS** (`photos.where_was(trip_segments, instant)`): the point of the segment active at that instant (a stay, rental or parking covering it; a train or cruise under way gives its port or city; a flight under way gives nothing); otherwise the destination of the last segment that ended within GAP hours before, else the origin of the next one starting within GAP hours after; else nothing. The result is stored with `location_source = inferred` and drawn as a hollow pin; it is never promoted to `exif`.

**Thresholds** (constants in `photomatch.py`, settled in #484):

| Name | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PAD` | 12 hours | Airport photos before a late-night flight home; the first morning after a trip's dates. |
| `HOME` | 2 km for an address or coordinates, 8 km for a ZIP or postal code, none for a city | The radius follows how precisely Home is known (`domain/home.py` knows which): a ZIP centroid can sit several kilometres from the house, and a city centroid cannot be trusted at all, so a fixed 2 km would let ordinary home photos through. |
| `NEAR` | 100 km | A day out from a campground or hotel is still "there". |
| `FAR` | 400 km | Beyond a long day's drive from every booking: still shown, but flagged, since the trip may be missing a stay. |
| `MARGIN` | 25 km | Two trips' nearest bookings within this of each other can't be told apart by GPS. |
| `GAP` | 36 hours | How long after checking out the itinerary still says where someone is. |
| `CLUSTER` | 5 km | Pins on the map: photos within this of a cluster's centroid join it. |

A re-sync re-runs the matcher on every `auto` row of a source whose granted trips changed (a trip gained a booking, dates moved, a grant was given or revoked), and leaves `owner` rows' trip alone, except when their trip is taken away (re-placing, Sync job).

## Sync job

`photosync.run(conn, source, provider, today, now)`; `jobs.py` runs `sync_all` every `PHOTO_SYNC_EVERY` (6 hours, first run 10 minutes after start) and a **Sync now** button runs one source in a thread like `scan_now`. One source syncs at a time; a source in `refused` or `off` is skipped until its owner acts. A source whose person has no active grant fetches nothing.

For each source:

1. Load the person's trips that have an active grant, and their windows. Drop `photo_trip_syncs` rows for trips that no longer qualify. Then re-place every row whose trip was taken away (its trip deleted or merged away, so `trip_id` is null and the match is not `ambiguous`; the person left it; or its grant was revoked and the revoke's own work was interrupted) and every row, `auto` or `owner`, that a changed window no longer holds: **re-placing** runs the matcher on the row as it is stored (instant, GPS), as `auto`, among the person's current candidates (less the trips its asset was removed from), and deletes the row only when no candidate is left and no still-granted trip whose window holds it has an exclusion for its asset; such a row is kept as a removed row on that trip (the first by trip id), so its removed list and **Put back** keep working. So a photo still inside another granted window moves there at once and is never left waiting for a fetch: an incremental fetch with `updatedAfter` would not return it again, and the weekly re-list is not needed for it. Stopping sharing, `photogrants.left`, a merge and a trip's deletion re-place their rows the same way in their own transaction, together with the person's `ambiguous` rows whose candidates included the trip taken away; this step only finishes what an interrupted one left, and since a paused or refused source never runs, nothing waits on it.
2. For each granted trip window: when the row is missing or the window moved, page `search/metadata` with `takenAfter`, `takenBefore`, `withExif: true`, `visibility: "timeline"`, `size: 1000`, `order: "asc"`, starting after `resume_after` when set (else from the window's start with `fetch_started = now`); an incremental run adds `updatedAfter: cursor`. Windows overlap between trips; a photo seen twice is one row. Page numbers shift when assets are added or removed during a traversal, so a traversal does not rely on them: it is keyset-paged. The first request of a window is page 1; every later one repeats the query with `takenAfter` set to the capture time of the last asset of the previous page (inclusive, so assets sharing that instant are seen again and de-duplicated by asset id) and page 1 again, so no amount of inserts or deletes before the boundary can shift an asset out of reach. If a whole page of 1000 shares one instant, which no stable cursor can step past, the window is marked too dense: it fetches what it can, never completes (so its cursor does not move and a re-list deletes nothing for it), and the source shows "Some of your library couldn't be read". `resume_after` stores that boundary, and a suspended traversal resumes from it. For each asset: upsert it: time fields, `asset_updated`, GPS, state and country, `seen = now`; run the matcher for `auto` rows with the candidates of Matching (less the trips each asset was removed from). Each page's writes commit in one transaction that first locks and re-reads the grants it writes under, the person's exclusions and the trip windows themselves (Consent, last point), writing nothing for a trip whose window no longer contains an asset or no longer matches the window the page was fetched under, so a stop or a removal made while the page was fetched wins. Each finished page stores the next `resume_after`; when `nextPage` is null the traversal is complete: `cursor = fetch_started`, `resume_after` and `fetch_started` cleared. `updatedAfter` only filters; it never resumes a traversal.
3. Every `FULL_RELIST_DAYS` (7) a source is fetched without cursors and, once the traversal has completed for every granted window of the source in that run (the windows are separate traversals, so rows belonging to a later window are not `seen` until it is fetched), rows not `seen` are deleted (trashed or deleted in Immich); an interrupted full re-list, or one that stopped before the last window, deletes nothing. A thumbnail or preview 404 from the proxy does not delete a row (see the proxy rules). Exclusions are never deleted by a re-list.
4. Record `last_sync` only when every window traversal of the run completed (a run stopped by `TIME_BUDGET` leaves `last_sync` as it was and Settings shows "Still reading your library" with the traversals left), and the counts (fetched, new, matched by kind, inferred, ambiguous, removed) in the log through `monitoring.log`, counts only, never per trip or per person.

What changes outside the sync, and what happens:

- **A grant is given, or a photo put back**: a sync of that person's sources for that trip starts at once (Consent).
- **Sharing is stopped**: the re-placing and deletions happen in its own transaction (Consent); the sync only finishes an interrupted one (step 1).
- **A person is added to a trip without a dialog** (a booking or traveller added by an email, an import or an assistant, where no confirmation can be shown, and `POST /api/segments` when it adds a traveller without growing the window): every Trip grant on that trip drops to Only me with `ask_again`, exactly as when the window grows, so the new person sees nothing of the others' photos until each owner is asked again; interactive additions show the photo warning and count first and ask the same way. `trips_changed` applies this in the change's own transaction.
- **A trip is split** (`POST /api/trips/{id}/split`): in the split's own transaction the moved bookings' dates leave the original trip's window and the new trip gets its own people and window. For each person, `photogrants.trips_changed` runs `left`/`joined`/`reconcile` for both trips; the original's rows outside its new window are re-placed or deleted (as a shrink), the new trip starts with no grants except the standing **Share on all my trips** setting's, and exclusions already recorded for the original are copied to the new trip whole (every exclusion of the original, rowless ones included, since an exclusion has no capture time to select by; an exclusion for an asset outside the new window does nothing), so a photo removed from the original does not reappear on the new trip if it is granted.
- **A person leaves a trip** (`trippeople` recomputed after a booking moves or is removed, or the person is taken off): in the same transaction, `photogrants.left(conn, person, trip)` deletes their `photo_grants` row for it (so if they are added again they start afresh, decision 14) and their sources' `photo_trip_syncs` rows for it, and re-places their `photos` rows on it, and, as in every path that removes a grant (Stop sharing, a person leaving, `end_lapsed`, a deleted trip), re-evaluates their tripless `home` rows: one whose capture time falls in no remaining granted window of the person is deleted, one still covered is kept. Their exclusions for the trip are kept, so a later re-grant still leaves those photos out.
- **A person is added to a trip**: with their **Share on all my trips** on, `photogrants.joined` grants it at their default scope and queues a sync (when the setting's `set_by` can see the trip); with it off, nothing: they have no grant and are asked when they first open it. Other people's grants on the trip are unchanged, and photos with scope Trip become visible to the newcomer because they are now one of the trip's people. Every path that adds someone to a trip's people shows the same photo lines and count in its confirmation before the change and before `joined` runs, including when the newcomer could already see the trip as its booker or a confirmation recipient: moving a booking into a trip and merging two trips (the move and merge previews and their dialogs in `Trip.svelte`), editing the trip's people (`PUT /api/trips/{id}` through `trips._set_people`, the people editor in `Trip.svelte`), and editing a booking's travellers so that `trippeople` gains someone (the booking editor's save). Each preview answers the newcomers and, for the confirming member: how many photos the newcomers will newly see: rows under a Trip grant and not excluded, that the confirming member may also see themselves; a confirming member's own or acted-for Only me photos are never counted, since newcomers will not see them, and two fixed lines, "People added to this trip may also see photos already shared on it." (shown always, since a confirming member who is only the trip's booker or a confirmation recipient sees none of its Trip photos and the count then misses exactly what newcomers gain) and "People added to this trip may also share their own photos here, as their own settings say.", which name no one and say nothing of anyone's setting or Only me photos (decision 17). Assistants already cannot move or merge between trips with different people.
- **A trip's window changes** (a booking added, moved or cancelled, dates edited). When it grows, every Trip grant on it drops to Only me in that change's own transaction, with `ask_again` set: the person keeps seeing their photos, everyone else stops seeing any of them, and the trip shows the person the sharing prompt again (with their count from the longer window), so nothing is shown to others for dates they did not agree to. This applies to every Trip grant, whatever the person's **Share on all my trips** says: the setting shares new trips, it is not consent to dates added later; the re-ask prompt shows even with the setting on. When it shrinks, rows outside the new window, `auto` and `owner` alike, are re-placed or deleted in the change's own transaction (Sync job, step 1). Either way the next run fetches the new window in full for each granted person, since the stored window no longer matches.
- **A trip is deleted**: `photogrants.trip_deleted(conn, trip)` runs in the deletion's own transaction, before the rows go: it re-places the trip's photos, `auto` and `owner` alike, and the `ambiguous` rows whose candidates included it, onto another granted trip whose window they fall in, else deletes them; then its grants, exclusions and `photo_trip_syncs` rows go with it (CASCADE). No row is left with a null `trip_id` for a later run, so a paused or refused source keeps nothing of a deleted trip.
- **Trips are merged**: see decision 12.

Budget: a page of 1000 assets with EXIF is a few megabytes; a two-year window on first fetch is tens of pages. `TIME_BUDGET` (10 minutes per source per run) stops a run cleanly after the current page; the next run continues from `resume_after`, as the mail scan resumes, and the update watermark moves only when the traversal finishes, so a large library is never left with assets skipped. 429 backs off; 401/403 sets `refused`; a network error sets `unreachable` and the status page says so.

Darkroom's rescans and sidecars change assets' `updatedAt`, so GPS added later arrives on the next incremental sync and the matcher re-runs for that row.

## Proxy and access rules

### Who sees a photo

One access predicate, `photos.visible(viewer)` in `waypoint/domain/photos.py`, a SQL condition over `photos` joined to `photo_sources`, `photo_grants` and `photo_exclusions`. Every path that shows a trip's photos, or anything built from them, applies it, and nothing else decides: no path has its own filter (the two person-only reads below have their own helpers and feed none of those paths). One asset can be reached through two of a person's sources (one Immich library connected twice, or a shared external library), which gives two rows. Every read keeps one canonical row per (person, asset id): the one with the lowest `source_id` among the person's sources in `ok` status, or the lowest overall when none is `ok`, chosen inside the helpers (deterministic, and a healthy library serves the pixels when one is refused), so the gallery, counts, clusters, the summary, Needs a look and the removed list show the photo once; an exclusion already covers both, being per (person, asset, trip). A viewer may see a photo when all of these hold:

1. The photo has a trip, and the viewer can see that trip (`visibility.visible_trip`).
2. The grant for (the photo's person, that trip) exists and `revoked` is null.
3. The viewer acts for the photo's person; or the grant's `scope` is `trip`, the viewer's own person is one of the trip's people (`trippeople.of`; seeing the trip as its booker or as a confirmation recipient is not enough, matching the label **Everyone on this trip**).
4. No `photo_exclusions` row exists for (the photo's person, its asset id, that trip).

`photos.visible` never returns an excluded row, an `ambiguous` row or a row with no trip, for anyone. Two other reads exist, each with its own helper and its own narrow check, and neither feeds any trip reply, count, cluster or summary, both only for someone acting for the photo's person: `photos.unsettled(viewer)` (a `far` row only when its trip is visible to the viewer, ambiguous candidates reduced to trips the viewer can see and the row dropped when none remain, like `visible` and `removed`), that person's `ambiguous` rows not left off and their `far` rows not yet kept or moved (each with its own actions: Pick a trip and Leave off for `ambiguous`; Keep on this trip, Pick a trip and Remove from this trip for `far`), for Needs a look and its image routes, with only the granted trips they fall in and were not removed from offered under **Pick a trip**; and `photos.removed(viewer, trip_id)`, that person's removed photos on a trip the viewer can see (an exclusion for that person and trip joined to their row by asset id), for the **Removed from this trip** list and its thumbnails only, through their own route `GET /api/trips/{id}/photo-exclusions/{photo_id}/thumbnail`. The image routes under `/api/photos/{id}/` answer `visible` or `unsettled`, never `removed`. `home` rows are kept (so a re-sync does not match them again) but served to no one; `none` rows are never written. There is no case for the trip's owner, a booker, an email recipient, the household admin, or an assistant.

Every path below is built from the rows this predicate returns for the viewer, and only from them:

- `GET /api/photos/{id}/thumbnail`, `/preview`, `/original`: 404 unless the predicate holds for that photo.
- `GET /api/trips/{id}/photos` (photos, clusters, counts, per-person counts): from the visible rows only. A person whose photos the viewer can't see does not appear in `people`, and no count, cluster size, cover, first or last time includes their photos.
- The map's pins and the route projection's fit, and the replay's lighting of pins: from that reply's clusters, so an invisible photo never widens the map or lights a pin.
- `GET /api/trips/{id}/summary`: the photo count, the inferred count, and the states and countries that photos add, from the visible rows only. A state only an invisible photo was taken in is not listed.
- `GET /api/photos/review` (Needs a look, which never lists a removed photo, which lives in each source card in Settings, Photos; the trip page only shows a one-line pointer to the person when the trip has `far` photos of theirs): only rows of persons the viewer acts for.
- `GET /api/photo-sources`: a source's counts ("1,904 photos on 12 trips") and last sync only to those who act for its person or connected it; everyone else sees its person, host and status, so a library's size says nothing about trips they can't see.
- `GET /api/trips/{id}/photo-sharing`, `GET /api/photo-grants` and the sharing preferences: only the grant state of persons the viewer acts for, on trips the viewer can see; no route tells anyone whether another person granted, declined, chose Only me, or has a library with photos from those dates.

So two people on the same trip can see different photos, different counts and a different set of places, and neither reply hints at the difference: the reply has the same shape and wording whether or not photos they can't see exist. A photo id the viewer may not see answers exactly like one that does not exist.

A Semgrep rule, `waypoint-photo-access`, fails a `select(Photo…)` or `Photo.` query in `waypoint/server/` or `waypoint/domain/` outside `photos.py`'s three helpers (`visible`, `unsettled`, `removed`), `photogrants.py`'s grants and removals and `photosync.py`'s writes, like `waypoint-trip-visibility` does for trips; `tripstats.py` asks `photos.visible_rows(conn, viewer, trip_id)` instead of its own query.

### Rules

- The photo id is Waypoint's; the handler finds the row through the predicate; a missing row, a photo the predicate refuses, a stranger or an assistant all get 404 with the same text (`NO_IMAGE`), and the check costs the same whichever it is (the row lookup and the predicate are one query).
- The upstream request goes through `providers/immich.py` with the decrypted key from `storage/photo_sources.py`; the key never appears in a URL, a log, an exception text or a reply header. Only the body and a sniffed content type come back; Immich's own headers are dropped, except the range headers of an original (below). Thumbnails and previews are `image/webp`, `image/jpeg` or `image/png`. Originals are checked against an allowlist by magic bytes, not by Immich's header: browser-renderable images (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, AVIF) and video (`video/mp4`, `video/quicktime`, `video/webm`) are served inline; HEIC/HEIF, TIFF, RAW and Matroska are served as `Content-Disposition: attachment` with their sniffed type or `application/octet-stream`; anything else, and always SVG and HTML, is 404. Every reply carries `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`.
- Caps: thumbnails 2 MB, previews 10 MB, read in chunks under a time budget (`mailimages.py` is the pattern); originals stream without a size cap under a 10-minute budget, and honour byte ranges: a valid single `Range` header is forwarded to Immich, and its `206`, `Content-Range`, `Content-Length` and `Accept-Ranges` come back, so a video seeks and resumes; a malformed or multi-range request is answered as a full body. The magic-byte check needs the file's first 4,096 bytes, so every range request is checked against a sniffed prefix first, whatever range it asks for (one starting later, or one as short as `bytes=0-1`): the type check of a full reply or of a `bytes=0-4095` fetch is kept in the process's memory for (photo id, `asset_updated`), and when there is none, Waypoint first fetches `bytes=0-4095` from Immich and checks that; only an allowed type lets the requested range through, so a seek never streams bytes no check has seen. Each range request runs the predicate again, and a stream in progress, full body or range, checks it again every 5 seconds while it sends: when the photo has been taken back the stream is cut off at once, so no original keeps leaving for more than 5 seconds after a removal, whatever the 10-minute budget allows. A 404 or 410 on a thumbnail or preview keeps the row (Immich may not have generated an external-library rendition yet) and answers 404 to the viewer, retryable; only when a follow-up `GET /api/assets/{id}` (`asset.read`) also answers 404 or 410 is the row deleted. 401/403 marks the source `refused` and answers 404 to the viewer.
- Cache policy, so that removal is immediate in browsers too: thumbnails and previews answer `Cache-Control: private, no-cache` with an ETag of (photo id, size, `asset_updated`). `no-cache` lets the browser keep the bytes but never show them without asking first; each use sends `If-None-Match`, the server runs the predicate, and answers `304` (no Immich request, so a gallery revisit stays cheap) when it holds and the ETag matches, `200` with the new rendition when the ETag changed, and `404` once the photo is removed. Originals answer `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. No reply carries `max-age`, `immutable` or `Expires`, and nothing is cacheable by a shared proxy. The web app keeps no photo in the Cache API, IndexedDB or a service worker (the offline vault holds none, below), and its in-memory object URLs for the lightbox are revoked when the photo is dropped. Plus `image_response`'s CSP (`default-src 'none'; sandbox`). The server never writes pixels to disk or the database.
- Assistants: `/api/photos`, `/api/photo-sources`, `/api/photo-grants`, `/api/photo-sharing-prefs` and the `/api/trips/{id}/photo-…` routes join `BLOCKED` in `mcp_access.py`; `/mcp` returns nothing about photos, grants or exclusions, not even counts.
- The offline copy (`GET /api/offline`) carries no photos, no photo counts, no grants and no exclusions, so a removal never waits on a device's saved copy.
- Sources: `GET /api/photo-sources` lists every source for the household (person, host, status; counts and last sync only as "Who sees a photo" says; `mine` for the viewer's own); `POST /api/photo-sources` creates one for the signed-in member themselves, or for a person in People with no sign-in when the caller is that person's `steward_sub`, or the person has no steward yet, which makes the caller their steward (Terms), atomically: the request locks the person's row (a self-assigning `UPDATE`, which waits on both Postgres and SQLite) before reading the sources and inserting, so of two members connecting at once the second finds a steward and is refused; any other person is refused; `POST /api/photo-sources/{id}` (key, label, paused) and `DELETE` answer only its `owner_sub`; `POST …/test` pings Immich with the key and reports the user it belongs to without saving anything; `POST …/sync` starts a run. **Connecting** a further library, or **resuming** a paused one, for a person who has an active Trip grant on a trip with photos in it asks once, and a newly connected library starts paused until the person answers ("Resume this library? N of its photos from trips you share with everyone will be added", **Cancel** / **Resume**), with N counted as in the sharing prompt and the request carrying the set of Trip grants and windows it counted (refused with 409 if any grant or window has changed, so a stale dialog can't enable the library under a newer widening), because the grant was consented against the libraries that were reading when it was given; Cancel on connecting leaves the library saved and paused. A new key replaces the old one only when it belongs to the same Immich user as the stored `immich_user_id`; the old one is never shown. Deleting a source deletes its photos (CASCADE); the person's grants, exclusions and preferences stay, so connecting the library again shares exactly what was shared before and nothing that was excluded.
- Lapsed access: `photos.end_lapsed` (in `jobs.sweep_lapsed`, next to `gmail.end_lapsed`) deletes a source whose `owner_sub` can no longer sign in.
- Demo and `make verify`: `demo.seed` adds a source per demo person with `url = "demo"` (no key), grants on the demo trips (one with scope Trip, one with Only me, one trip with no grant so the prompt shows, one declined so the reminder shows, and removed photos) and photo rows across the granted trips; for a `demo` source the proxy serves a deterministic generated PNG (`photos.placeholder(asset_id, size)`, a gradient tile like `demo._png`) and the prompt's count comes from `demo.unshared_inventory(person, trip)`, a fixed number per demo person and ungranted trip with no stored rows behind it (an ungranted trip stores none), and neither ever opens a connection, so `make verify` runs with its network blocked and the pages still show photos.

## Map and stats

**Map.** The trip page already draws the route from the segments (`RouteReplay.svelte` → `GET /api/trips/{id}/places` → `domain/tripmap.py`: airports, stays, road legs along US highways, state lines). Photos add a layer, not a second map: `GET /api/trips/{id}/photos` returns the photos the viewer may see and their clusters, and `RouteReplayMap.svelte` draws a pin per cluster (size by count, hollow when every photo in it is inferred), projected with the same `routeProjection`, which is extended to fit the stops and the cluster coordinates together (a trip with no stops fits the clusters alone; a single cluster gets a fixed regional zoom), so a `far` cluster hundreds of kilometres away is still inside the map. During a replay a cluster lights up when the replay passes its first photo's time (the replay already tracks `stopDates`). Tapping a pin scrolls the gallery to that cluster. Today the map shows only for a trip that has ended with two placeable stops; with photos it shows for any trip with at least one cluster the viewer may see or two stops, and the replay controls appear only when the route is replayable. Because clusters come from the viewer's visible rows, whether the map appears at all, its extent and its pins never reveal photos the viewer can't see; when a removal empties the clusters, the next read of the reply drops the pins and, for a trip with fewer than two stops, the map.

**Clusters** are built server-side in `photos.clusters(rows)`, per request, from the predicate's rows for the viewer: greedy by time order, a photo joins the first cluster whose centroid is within CLUSTER km, else starts one; each cluster carries centroid, count, first and last local time, cover photo id and whether it is all-inferred. The reply caps clusters at 400 (merging the smallest neighbours past that), so a two-year trip stays drawable on a phone. Nothing about clusters is stored.

**Stats** (`domain/tripstats.py`, `GET /api/trips/{id}/summary`): days (trip dates), nights away (union of stay and cruise nights, as `stats._nights`), distance (flights: great-circle between airports via `stats.distance_km`; road: `tripmap.road_legs` miles where a highway route exists, else straight-line between consecutive placed stays, labelled "about"), states and countries (from segment addresses and visit details via `geocode`/`regions`, plus the `state`/`country` of the trip's photos that the viewer may see, from Immich, leaving out `far` photos until their person accepts or moves them, so one wrong-clock or unrelated photo cannot add a place to the trip; US states listed by name, other countries by name), photo count with how many are inferred (the viewer's visible photos), and bookings by kind. The web app shows distance in the household's `distance_unit` as Stats does. The segment-based figures are the same for everyone who can see the trip; the photo-based ones are per viewer.

## Decisions

Settled by the owner in #484 (1–12; 3, 4, 9, 10 and 12 revised by #504), in #504 and its designs (13–21), and afterwards (22, and the confirmation in 9 and 21).

1. **Thresholds**: the table above.
2. **A photo that fits two overlapping trips**: GPS decides when it can (MARGIN); otherwise the shorter trip; a tie waits for its person under "Needs a look". Only granted trips compete, less the ones the photo was removed from.
3. **Hiding**: superseded by **Remove from this trip** (decision 15). There is no separate hidden state, and 0039 drops `photos.hidden`. Nobody else can remove another person's photo, and the trip's owner cannot remove someone else's photo.
4. ~~**Who sees whose photos**: everyone who can see a trip sees all its photos, from every library.~~ Superseded by #504: a person's photos are used on a trip only with their grant, and seen by others only under the Trip scope (decisions 13–17).
5. **Sync**: every 6 hours, a full re-list weekly, Sync now per source; first run 10 minutes after start; a grant starts a sync of that trip at once.
6. **Originals through Waypoint**: yes, streamed, needing `asset.download` on the key, under the same predicate and with `no-store`.
7. **Videos**: included (thumbnail from Immich, original streamed on tap).
8. **Archived photos**: skipped (`visibility: timeline` only).
9. **Who may connect a library for whom**: narrowed by #504 from #484's "any signed-in member for any person in People". Now a person with a sign-in connects their own libraries only, and nobody else can connect one for them; a person without a login (a child) has one steward, who connects all of their libraries (Terms; the owner confirmed the steward rule), and a second member connecting a library for that person is refused. This narrowing is new in this revision, for the owner to see: it stops one member attaching their own Immich account to another person whose grants would then share it. Connecting a library shares nothing: its person's grants decide, and for a person without a login the steward shares for them.
10. **`far` photos**: shown on the trip with a flag (to those the predicate allows), and listed under "Needs a look" for their person with **Keep on this trip**, **Pick a trip** and **Remove from this trip**; `ambiguous` photos get **Pick a trip** and **Leave off**.
11. **States crossed**: Immich's reverse-geocoded state per photo plus the booking addresses is enough. State lines crossed by the route are not counted from photos in v1, a scope choice; counting them would be a later issue.
12. **Merging trips**: the merged-away trip's photos become unassigned and its grants go with it, because its people become people on the surviving trip, and a person added to a trip is treated as decision 14 says (shared by their standing setting, else asked). Its exclusions move to the surviving trip (the same person and asset, deduplicated), so a removed photo stays removed. The merge re-places the unassigned rows, `auto` and `owner` alike, in its own transaction (Sync job, step 1): a photo inside another granted window of its person moves there at once, the rest are deleted and fetched again when its person grants the surviving trip (a grant fetches the window in full). A photo placed by hand on the merged-away trip is therefore matched afresh, not kept by hand. The merge itself carries no photos over.
13. **Per-trip grant**: each person must grant access, per trip, for their photos to be used on it. Without an active grant their photos on that trip are not fetched, matched, shown, counted, pinned or in the trip summary, for anyone, including them.
14. **When it is asked**: the first time a person opens a trip that has photos in their library, with Only me preselected, **Share on this trip**, **Share on all my trips** and **Not now** (design i). **Share on all my trips** is a standing setting (`photo_sharing_prefs.share_all`): while on, trips the person joins later are shared at their default scope without asking; while off, a person added to a trip later starts unshared and is asked, and so does anyone whose grant was deleted when they left a trip.
15. **Remove from this trip**: a person can remove single photos from a trip they shared, from the viewer or several at once from the gallery; removed photos are never shown, counted or pinned for anyone, stay removed across syncs, re-lists, stopping and sharing again, and a library reconnected, and are listed for their person alone with **Put back**. The name is **Remove from this trip** everywhere and replaces **Hide** (decision 3); an `ambiguous` photo in Needs a look gets **Leave off**. The trip's owner cannot remove someone else's photo.
16. **Scope**: each grant is Trip (the other people on the trip, `trippeople.of`, see the photos; when someone else's change makes the trip longer, the grant drops to Only me and the person is asked again, whatever their Share on all my trips says; a booker or confirmation recipient who is not one of them does not, so the label **Everyone on this trip** stays true and the designs are unchanged) or Only me; the default is Only me. The trip's owner cannot see Only me photos; nobody but those acting for the photo's person can.
17. **No hint of photos you can't see**: other people on a trip cannot tell that someone has photos they cannot see: counts, per-person counts, pins, clusters, the map's extent, the replay and the summary never reveal them, and no route reports another person's grant state.
18. **Removal is always possible and immediate**: whoever shared a photo can take it back, a single photo (**Remove from this trip**), a whole trip (**Stop sharing on this trip**) or by switching to Only me, at any time, and it takes effect at once for everyone and every path: thumbnails, previews, originals and ranges, the trip photos reply, clusters and pins, the replay, the summary and its counts, Needs a look, the offline copy and `/mcp` (which never carry photos). Waypoint keeps the exclusion (and the removed photo's row, for its person's **Put back**) or the revoked grant, so a re-sync cannot bring the photo back, and stopping deletes the derived rows. Narrowing to Only me saves without asking, with Undo; widening and stopping ask once.
19. **Cache policy**: thumbnails and previews `private, no-cache` with the ETag revalidated through the predicate on every use (404 once removed); originals `private, no-store`; no photo in any browser storage; open pages fetch the photo list again on focus and every 60 seconds while visible.
20. **The trip's owner gets no special access**: there is no owner case in the predicate, and they cannot remove someone else's photo; Needs a look and every photo action are for those acting for the photo's person.
21. **A library for someone without a login** is shared by whoever connected it, their steward, who is the only member who can connect their libraries; a second member connecting one for that person is refused (confirmed by the owner).
22. **Local mode (no sign-in)**: photos are off. There are no photo sources, grants or sharing in local mode, and the UI shows none of it.

## Open questions

- Should `get_trip` over `/mcp` say how many photos a trip has? Left out of v1; if it is added, the count must come from `photos.visible` for the connection's member, never a trip-wide total.
- Should the household Stats page (`domain/stats.py`) count photos per year? Left out of v1; the same rule would apply (the viewer's visible photos only).

Should a restore keep the current revocations and exclusions (and Only me switches) where they are newer, or is a warning on the restore screen enough?
