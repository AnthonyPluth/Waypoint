The Offline access card in the iPhone app

Inside the iPhone app, saving trips is native: no passkey, no Face ID, and the copy lives in the app’s private storage. This is the Settings card that turns saving on and off, and every state it can be in. Dark only, vector only. Use the toggle for phone width (390 px) or desktop width (960 px for the cards, 1280 px for the page in place). Nothing is built until you sign this off; the open decisions are in the last section and in the README.

In place: Settings, Offline & notifications

The card keeps the app’s existing rows pattern and sits in the same place, first in the Offline & notifications group, above the reminders. Inside the iPhone app it replaces both the passkey card and the “isn’t available” card. Shown here in its most common state (saved, two trips). The states follow in A to G.

A

Off

The first state every iPhone is in: saving is off until the person turns the switch on, because the app’s saving flag defaults to off. There is no setup sheet and no device check; the switch is the whole action. The same card appears after the person switches saving off (nothing is kept then).

B

Turning on, and saving

Turning the switch on sets the app’s saving flag at once, then the app fetches the trips. While that runs the switch is disabled and the line says Saving your trips…, then the card becomes C or D. Turning the switch off is immediate and deletes the saved trips, as in the browser (no confirmation).

C

Saved, with the trips and when

The saved trips are named with the time of the last save (“12 min ago”, same wording as the browser card), and a line says when each is removed. Remove from this iPhone asks first (C3). The list and its expand control for many trips (C4, C5) are a proposal.

D

On, nothing saved yet or any more

Saving is on but no trip is kept: a new phone, no upcoming trip, or every saved trip ended more than 3 days ago and was removed. The same words cover all three. There is nothing to remove, so the Remove row is not shown.

E

Problems

A failed save keeps the older copy (the app never replaces a good copy with nothing). A copy that can’t be read can only be removed. Both need the app to report them; see question 6.

F

Signed out

Settings can’t be opened while signed out, so there is no card. Signing out deletes the saved trips, and the app’s own sign-out screen is what the person sees. The extra line in amber is a proposal; the rest is the screen as it is today.

G

Not supported

Two cases. G1 is a browser, including Safari on an iPhone: the card is exactly as today, and its wording is not touched. G2 is an installed app too old to have the offline bridge: it can’t tell itself apart from a browser, so it would show G1’s passkey card; G2 is a proposal for what to show instead, and needs question 9 answered.

H

Upcoming and the trip page

These panels read the same state as the card and keep their layout; only the wording changes inside the app (“this iPhone” for “this device”, no Face ID, no passkey). The green Saved for offline pill is unchanged. Shown in the order saved, trip not saved, off, nothing yet, can’t be read.

Open decisions for the owner

None of these is decided. Where a frame above shows an answer, it is marked as a proposal or named in the question.

  1. What should Remove from this iPhone do while the switch is on?The app’s Remove deletes the saved copy and leaves saving on, so the next online open would save the trips again. The design shows option A.
    • A. Remove also switches saving off (drawn; two calls from the web, no native change). Removing then means “stop and delete”.
    • B. Remove deletes and keeps saving on; the trips come back at the next online open. The dialog would say so.
    • C. No Remove button: the switch is the only control, because switching off already deletes.
  2. Does the card say “this iPhone” or “this device”?The iPhone app may also run on an iPad. “This iPhone” reads warmly on a phone and is wrong on an iPad; “this device” is the browser card’s word.
  3. Does the card mention backups?You decided the saved files may go into iCloud and computer backups. The card says so in one quiet line (“The saved copy can be part of your iCloud and computer backups.”). Keep it, move it into the docs only, or drop it?
  4. Should the app offer saving the first time a trip opens?In the browser a sheet offers “Keep this trip available offline” on the first trip. The app as drawn has no prompt: saving is only in Settings and the person has to find it. Add a one-time offer, or leave it in Settings?
  5. Is saving off or on for a new install?Today it starts off (A) and the person turns it on. Alternatively it could start on at the first sign-in, given that nothing leaves the phone and there is no Face ID step.
  6. May the app report progress and failures to the card?States B (saving) and E (couldn’t save) need the status reply to carry a “saving now” flag and a “last save failed” flag. The bridge has neither today (status returns only the saving flag, when and which trips). Add them (a small change to #737 and #739’s Swift), or show only A, C and D and say nothing about a failed save?
  7. Does the signed-out screen say the saved trips were removed?F adds one line to the native screen. It is true, and it tells a person who is about to board that the trips are gone; it is a change to a screen outside this issue.
  8. After signing in again, is saving on or off?Sign-out deletes the trips and the saving flag is per phone. Does the person find the switch still on (and the trips come back at the next open) or reset to off (A)?
  9. What does an app too old for the offline bridge show?It can’t be told from a browser (G2). Options: leave the passkey or “isn’t available” card there as today (G1), show G2’s update note, or detect the app from its user agent and show nothing at all.
  10. Does the wide layout matter?0b shows desktop width. Is the app expected to run on an iPad or a Mac, or is phone width the only one that ships?
  11. How should ten saved trips be listed?The issue says the card names the trips. Up to ten can be saved. C4 and C5 show three names and “and 7 more”, with Show all and Show fewer. The alternative is always listing all of them, or only “10 trips saved”.
  12. Is the privacy sentence right?“Kept in Waypoint’s private storage on this iPhone, protected by the phone’s lock. Other apps and websites can’t read it, and Waypoint adds no encryption of its own.” It must be true for the owner’s decisions (sandbox, Data Protection .complete, no Face ID) and plain for a traveller. Reword it?

Notes

Accessible names and what is reused.

Accessible names

  • Section: Offline access (heading, with the group’s section landmarks as today).
  • Status line: On for this iPhone, Off on this iPhone, Couldn’t save the trips, The saved trips can’t be read. The chip repeats the state in words, never by colour alone.
  • Switch: role="switch", name Save my upcoming trips, 44 px touch target. Disabled while saving.
  • Remove: Remove from this iPhone; its confirmation is an alertdialog titled Remove the saved trips? with Cancel focused first and Remove.
  • Saving line is aria-live="polite"; the result (saved or failed) is announced there.

Tokens and components

  • Only existing tokens: --card, --border, --primary, --status-ok-*, --status-bad-*, --muted-foreground, --destructive. No new token.
  • Components: the app’s rows and row, Button (outline), the existing switch input, ConfirmDialog and the status chip as in the other Settings cards.
  • Icons are the app’s lucide set: lock, hard-drive-download, loader-circle.

What does not change

  • In a browser, the card, the passkey sheet and every word are as today.
  • No Face ID, no passkey, no device check anywhere in the app’s card.
  • Reminders and the calendar subscription below the card.
  • Every name, trip and time in these frames is made up.
